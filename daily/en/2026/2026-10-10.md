### Cloudflare adds audio and video to its decision models, while its cheaper hosted option gets a shorter context window

Cloudflare introduced Clef-omni on October 9, allowing text, images, audio, and video to enter a structured decision task in a single request. It targets classification, moderation, and extraction, rather than general-purpose generative chat. It is based on Qwen3-Omni-30B-A3B-Instruct, with the speech-output path removed.

The same update cuts Clef-flash’s input price from $0.09 to $0.038 per million tokens, while reducing its Workers AI hosted context window from 64k to 24k. The weights are unchanged, and the maximum context for self-hosting remains 256k. Clef-omni costs $0.15 per million input tokens. It does not lead on every task in the company’s tests: invoice exact-match results, for example, are below the original Clef’s. Latency and performance figures also depend on the specified tasks and operating conditions. Model selection needs to consider input modalities, context, price, and the cost of incorrect decisions together.

**Original title:** Introducing Clef-omni with full multimodality, plus a faster Clef and a cheaper Clef-flash

<!-- item:publisher-ai -->
### Publishing workers challenge undisclosed AI use, highlighting the distinction between efficiency tools and creative authorization

WIRED’s October 9 reporting interviews more than 20 anonymous publishing workers about large language models being used inside HarperCollins, Simon & Schuster, and Hachette to draft emails, publicity copy, and back-cover copy, as well as for cover design. Employees’ accounts do not fully align with publishers’ public positions. Anonymous testimony should not be expanded into a claim of uniform practice across the entire industry.

Hachette said it supports operational uses that help promote authors’ books, but does not support creative uses, including communications with authors and partners. Simon & Schuster said employees can use a small number of vetted enterprise tools, without a requirement to do so; HarperCollins did not respond. The dispute includes whether authors know about these uses, whether unpublished material may be entered into models, and how tools affect the distribution and recognition of work. Data flows through enterprise tools, isolated deployments, and ordinary online chat must be distinguished. The report does not establish that all upload paths are equally safe or equally risky.

**Original title:** Book Publishers Are Quietly Using More AI. Staff Are Revolting

<!-- item:gpu-scheduling -->
### Ai2 schedules GPUs with time budgets and explains the trade-offs for interactive work

The Ai2 team published a scheduling account on Hugging Face on October 9. It allocates GPU time budgets across a hierarchy and combines minimum runtime guarantees, time slicing, and requeueing to reduce prolonged resource occupation and escalating priorities. The team reports a 74% reduction in repairs requiring human intervention. That is a result from its own deployment, not a throughput improvement established for every cluster.

The authors also explain that some work does not improve: interactive sessions can be preempted after their guaranteed time, leaving users to reconstruct volatile state, while large jobs can suffer from resource fragmentation. CPU development clusters and resumable sessions remain future plans. Scheduling should be judged by what gets completed and how much time recovery consumes, alongside GPU utilization.

**Original title:** Impactful scheduling for GPU clusters

## Research

<!-- item:agentmon -->
### AgentMonBench connects an agent’s important choices to source evidence

The October 5 preprint, revisited after a Hugging Face recommendation on October 9, constructs three software-engineering subsets: omitted requirements; changes in code semantics that still pass existing tests; and autonomous choices in real conversations that users later contest. Evidence-Grounded Behavior Graph (EBG) organizes evidence from code or execution traces through deterministic procedures. Semantic judgments still come from the monitoring model.

Across eight models, the authors report improvements in decision recognition and evidence localization in most settings, but not on every metric. Five controlled cases show how the approach can expose issues such as ambiguous goals, data leakage, or cached responses passed off as live checks. It helps users inspect important choices, without guaranteeing that agents never make mistakes or providing independent verification across all production tasks. It was selected because it directly addresses the question raised by today’s news: which step did the system take, and what evidence supported it?

**Original title:** What Did the Agent Actually Do? Evidence-Grounded Oversight for Long-Horizon Agents

<!-- item:testprism -->
### TestPrism asks tests to reject faulty implementations and accept different correct ones

The October 8 preprint presents 300 testing tasks from 17 sources, each with ten labeled implementations, evenly split between correct and incorrect ones. Joint Success Function requires tests to fail on the initial program, accept every correct implementation, and reject every incorrect implementation. Across fourteen baseline configurations, the strongest joint success rate is 28%, while the same configuration reaches 59.67% under single-reference evaluation.

TestHelix cross-checks different test and repair proposals, reviews their semantics, and iterates on strategies. In the specified comparisons across two models, it improves results by 8.67 to 9 percentage points. A finite implementation panel does not exhaust every semantic possibility or environment, and the balanced sample is not a real-world error distribution. This work helps distinguish passing tests from tests that actually protect requirements; it does not establish the overall quality of real projects.

**Original title:** TestPrism: Rethinking Test Evaluation Beyond a Single Reference

### Deno joins Cloudflare, with different transition plans for the runtime and hosting service

Ryan Dahl announced on October 9 that the team is joining Cloudflare and will build a platform around Workers and Durable Objects. The Deno runtime is planned to receive another year of maintenance, with monthly bug and security fixes, after which the team will stop active development. Its source remains open for the community to maintain. Deno Deploy will operate for another six months before shutting down; paying users need to migrate according to the announcement. JSR will continue running, supported by Cloudflare infrastructure.

This does not mean every project shuts down immediately, nor that cloud services will keep operating indefinitely in their current form. Users need to check their dependencies on runtime versions, hosted deployments, and package registries separately, then plan migrations. The team sets out a platform direction; the announcement does not demonstrate a uniform performance improvement for agent workloads.

**Original title:** Deno is joining Cloudflare
