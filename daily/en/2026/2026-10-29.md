### Google releases Gemini 4 Carbon, and at first gives it only to network defenders

On October 28, Google DeepMind announced the frontier model Gemini 4 Carbon and said it is the company's strongest model for long-horizon tasks and cybersecurity. On the company's published tests, Carbon scores 88.2 percent on the vulnerability-finding benchmark SEC-bench Pro, 54.8 percent on the exploit benchmark ExploitGym, and 71.0 percent on the patching benchmark CWE-bench. The company also says it ranks first on the Artificial Analysis Intelligence Index v5.0 with a score of 59, that the successful-cheat rate on Terminal-Bench 4.0 fell to 0.0 percent, and that high-severity mismatch alerts in an internal coding environment fell by nearly 80 percent. Those are the company's own tests. External benchmarks were also run without safety guardrails.

Unlike earlier open releases, Google says it is taking part in a voluntary US government pre-release model test. This week it opens the model first to a group of network defenders, then widens it through the Fairwind Program to trusted partners. The time for developers, companies, and consumers has not been announced. The price matches Gemini 4 Argon, released two weeks earlier: 2 dollars per million input tokens and 10 dollars per million output tokens. The point to watch is whether staged release and third-party testing before release become a normal path for frontier models, once attack and defense capability rise together.

[Google DeepMind | October 28, 2026](https://deepmind.google/blog/gemini-4-carbon-our-next-frontier-model/)

### Microsoft states Azure revenue in dollars for the first time, and records 10 billion dollars from OpenAI in one quarter

Microsoft reported results on October 28 for the quarter ended September 30. TechCrunch reports that Microsoft for the first time stated Azure quarterly revenue directly in dollars: 33.2 billion dollars from July through September, up 48 percent, above the 44 to 45 percent chief financial officer Amy Hood had expected, and about 36 percent of the company's 92.3 billion dollars of total revenue. Azure sits in the new Agents and Infra segment for the quarter.

TechCrunch also cites Microsoft's regulatory filing that day: Microsoft recorded 10 billion dollars of revenue from its commercial arrangements with OpenAI, including revenue share, about 11 percent of total revenue. For fiscal 2026 the figure is 24.1 billion dollars. Receivables from OpenAI at quarter end were 7.2 billion dollars, against 6.0 billion at the end of June. Microsoft's stake in OpenAI fell from about 25 percent in June to about 24 percent in September. The filing does not split how much of the 10 billion dollars is revenue share and how much is Azure cloud service. The partnership is no longer exclusive, but the financial tie is still deep.

### Anthropic ships team-usage reports, answering companies that ask what the AI spending bought

TechCrunch reported on October 28 that Anthropic introduced reporting tools so a company manager can see, over a period, what a team did with AI, what it produced, what it spent, and where the workflow stuck. The data comes from internal analytics and employee surveys. Anthropic says it will not by default use this data to train models. The reports also offer improvement suggestions, and a shared team budget pool is added for token spending.

The background is that companies are asking about return. The report says Anthropic told investors this week that it reached operating profit in the second quarter, with net revenue retention of 900 percent. A report from the software-engineering analytics company Jellyfish says coding tools based on Anthropic models submitted nearly one third of changes awaiting review, that spending on such tools has grown about tenfold since the start of the year, and that it is about 2 percent of total engineering cost. In the words of Jellyfish's chief scientist, the spending growth has already exceeded most finance leaders' budgets, and the next job is to make it actually produce an effect. That sentence is a paraphrase of the reported remark, not a verbatim quotation.

[TechCrunch | October 28, 2026](https://techcrunch.com/2026/10/28/anthropic-just-launched-tools-to-show-companies-what-their-ai-spending-is-buying/)

### Google opens Gemini and Search to more apps, and Chrome autofill can read Gmail

On October 28, Google launched a preview of the Connected Apps Platform, open for applications from developers in the United States. Companies and developers can connect through a cloud-hosted MCP server or an Android MCP based on the AppFunctions interface, register, test, and submit an app in the Google Connected Console, and choose to appear in Search, the Gemini app, or both. After launch they can see traffic, latency, and error rate. TechCrunch says Gemini already has more than 50 connected apps, including partners such as Adobe, Experian, and Peloton.

The same day, Chrome's enhanced autofill started using Gemini with Personal Intelligence enabled to pull order numbers, tracking numbers, and flight and hotel bookings from the user's Gmail into forms. It launches first in the United States, on desktop, Android, and iOS. The user can manage the access in settings or turn it off. The two moves point the same way. People increasingly call services through an AI assistant. How an app is seen by the assistant, and how far personal data is pulled, become a new competitive point and a privacy question.

[TechCrunch | October 28, 2026](https://techcrunch.com/2026/10/28/as-ai-agents-reshape-the-web-google-opens-gemini-and-search-to-more-apps/)

### Data-center resistance hardens: an xAI subsidiary offers to buy nearby homes if owners drop claims

The Verge reported on October 28 that xAI's subsidiary MZX is offering to buy homes near a data center and power plant in Southaven, Mississippi, on the condition that owners sign an agreement waiving all claims related to litigation against the facility. The report quotes a broker who went door to door saying the plan is to buy 418 homes and that 18 households intend to sell. In a letter to residents, MZX denies all allegations in the litigation.

Another report the same day focuses on Texas. A poll cited in the report shows 57 percent of Texans oppose building data centers: 43 percent of Republicans, 75 percent of Democrats, and 54 percent of independents. Gary Bledsoe, president of the Texas NAACP, estimates that the anti-data-center alliance We the People Texas, including his organization, represents as many as one million voters, concerned about water, tax subsidies, and new power plants. The bottleneck in compute expansion is moving from chips and electricity to local consent.

### NVIDIA opens Nemotron 3.5 Transcribe: one model for transcription, translation, and speaker separation

NVIDIA released the open-weight speech model Nemotron 3.5 Transcribe on Hugging Face. The same checkpoint supports transcription in 40 languages, translation between 31 of them and English, speaker separation in 13 languages, and audio question answering. A single recording can run up to about one hour and label up to 8 speakers, and a natural-language prompt can control the output format. Released with the model are the Granary v2 data pipeline and a fine-tuning recipe. NVIDIA reports that in one case, fine-tuning cut the word-error rate for unseen speakers by 45.8 percent relative to the best prompting setup. Those are the publisher's tests. License terms follow the model page.

### MLE-World: predict the result of a machine-learning experiment before running it

This preprint, submitted to arXiv on October 27, addresses the cost of an AI research agent that has to really train once for every idea it tries. It proposes MLE-World, a world model fine-tuned from Qwen3.5-4B. Looking only at code and a data description, it predicts the program's errors, training logs, and validation score, and gives a confidence. An agent can compare several candidates first and really execute only when confidence is low. The authors report that on 17 MLE-bench Lite tasks, its predictions matched real outputs for held-out programs 77.9 percent of the time, against 41.1 percent for a larger prompted model. Plans guided by it improved average performance by up to 22.1 percent with fewer real executions. Those are the authors' results on a specific task set. The paper has not been peer reviewed.

[arXiv | v1 submitted October 27, 2026, at 13:01 UTC](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.37175)

### Satya Nadella: spread it widely, and bring in third-party testing

Satya Nadella is chair and chief executive of Microsoft. He joined Microsoft in 1992, became chief executive in 2014, and previously led cloud and enterprise. All-In is a business and technology discussion show. The conversation collected here is about the pace of AI, Microsoft's strategy, and industry competition.

In this mid-September 2026 conversation, Nadella says the value of AI is that the technology spreads across industries, which requires choice and competition. He also says he strongly favors bringing in third-party testing organizations, and compares current frontier models to an empirical science that needs controlled experiments. This is a view from more than a month ago, not a response to today's news. Set against this issue: Google gave Carbon a staged release and joined a government pre-release test, while Microsoft's results show a deep financial tie to OpenAI. How to hold diffusion and testing together is the question those two stories share. This is a paraphrase, not a verbatim quotation.
