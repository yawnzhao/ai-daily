# AI Daily Digest No. 43 — Power for Compute, Robotics, and the Business of Content

October 19, 2026, Beijing time. AI expansion is increasingly reflected in concrete business decisions. A large British data-center project has lost Microsoft as a major customer over power supply; a Chinese embodied-AI startup is building around touch and real production data; and AI animated-drama teams are reconsidering how to make money after increasing output. Meanwhile, retailers are using AI to assist pricing, and Anthropic has proposed using company shares to support public benefit.

The connecting theme is how AI works as a business. Its reach depends on model capabilities alongside electricity, production processes, user demand, and the distribution of income.

### Microsoft leaves a British data-center project as electricity constrains expansion

The Guardian reported on October 18 that Microsoft had withdrawn from Nscale's data-center project in Loughton, Essex, over power-supply arrangements. Microsoft had been a major customer for the British company's project. The facility was scheduled to open next year, but the report cited the grid company's information that sufficient power might not arrive until the early-to-mid 2030s.

Nscale said it would continue with the project, exploring alternative power sources and other British locations. Its other contracts with Microsoft are unaffected by this grid issue. The immediate change therefore concerns a particular project, rather than an end to cooperation between Microsoft and Nscale overall.

The case brings an often-overlooked part of AI construction into view. Even after obtaining chips and signing customers, a facility needs enough electricity connected on time. Buildings, servers, power, and customer start dates must align; a delay in one can leave the other investments waiting.

For the data-center industry, the next questions are whether alternative power can be secured, how delivery schedules change, and where the departing customer's demand goes. These developments affect both the destination of orders and when compute actually enters service.

### 格物通智 raises angel funding, approaching robotic manipulation through touch

On October 18, Yingke reported that Hangzhou embodied-AI company 格物通智 had completed an angel funding round. The company combines tactile, visual, and other data with physics-inversion world models and self-evolving techniques to develop robotic manipulation for industrial settings. The funding is intended for model training, technical iteration, hardware development, and collecting data from deployment scenarios.

Its chosen problem is specific: after a robot sees an object, it also needs to understand what happens when its hand touches it. Fabric slips, insertion meets resistance, and flexible materials change shape as they move. Fine manipulation requires feeding this contact information back into control and learning systems.

The report says the company signed a strategic cooperation agreement with 匹克投资 to pursue applications in footwear manufacturing. Shoes involve both flexible materials and fine manipulation, translating model questions into production-floor questions: whether grasping is stable, whether a sequence of operations can be completed, and how much adjustment a different material requires.

The case illustrates growing differentiation among embodied-AI startups. Some build general-purpose models; others try to enter through contact sensing, a particular material, or a production step. For the latter, a deployment setting offers both potential customers and data for continued learning. How training capability and delivery capability reinforce each other will matter more for business progress than an isolated demonstration.

### As AI animated dramas grow longer, production teams recalculate returns

A set of industry interviews published on October 18 describes multi-season serials and very long stories becoming production formats for some leading AI animated dramas. AI makes characters, settings, voices, and workflows easier to reuse, while long online novels provide stories for continued adaptation, enabling faster production of another season.

Rising output makes the economics clearer. Producers interviewed said that income from successful titles could be absorbed by investment in other projects. Boutique teams repeatedly refine shots and storytelling, while content factories bear the cost of many underperforming titles. Reducing production cost per minute addresses only part of the cost structure.

Platform incentives are changing too. The report says 红果 removed an additional series-scale multiplier in its September incentive-rule update. From October, series no longer receive that extra uplift, although eligible sequels can still earn rewards based on their own performance. Teams reliant on serialization must therefore recalculate their income structure.

Some content factories are reducing centralized production and instead offering tools, compute, and distribution support to outside creators. Small teams with successful titles are gradually hiring and building sustained delivery capacity. These seemingly opposite changes address the same question: how to turn ongoing investment into work audiences want to watch, and then into income the team can retain.

AI's production efficiencies are already part of everyday operations in this industry. The next differences will emerge from how topic selection, storytelling, distribution, and organization work together. Faster production makes judgments about what to create and how far to develop it more consequential.

[Industry interviews | 叁拾代, syndicated with permission by 36Kr, October 18](https://www.36kr.com/p/4030064430684039)

### US retailers adopt AI-assisted pricing as consumers question how prices are set

An October 18 Associated Press report examines changing pricing practices among US retail and restaurant businesses. Electronic shelf labels replace paper tags, while algorithms combine sales, competitors' prices, demand, and other information to help businesses decide when and how much to change prices. AI further improves their ability to find patterns in these data.

Consumers' central concern is whether the same product will be priced differently for different people. Several mechanisms need to be distinguished. Dynamic pricing changes with market conditions such as supply and demand. Personalized pricing uses information about individuals or groups. Electronic labels are tools for displaying and updating prices. Installing them alone does not establish that a store has begun pricing by individual customer.

The report presents different views from businesses, consumer researchers, and regulators. Retailers emphasize efficiency and timely updates; consumers question how their data are used and whether price changes are transparent. Some states have restricted pricing based on personal data, and the controversy has also entered litigation.

The significance is that AI commercialization reaches beyond chat interfaces into the price systems shoppers encounter daily. Businesses may benefit from more granular demand estimates. For consumers, trust depends on understanding price changes and how equivalent transactions are treated.

[Original report | Associated Press, October 18](https://apnews.com/article/ai-prices-dynamic-customers-technology-64c446cd117d3be664de4ab570fb9857)

### Anthropic proposes contributing company shares to a public-benefit fund

On October 18, Anthropic announced the Anthropic Public Benefit Fund, proposing to contribute 5% of the company's shares, subject to shareholder approval. For each 50% increase in the company's valuation above a specified baseline, the board could also choose to contribute another 2% in shares.

Potential uses include donations to nonprofits and direct distributions of resources to particular groups to address AI-related employment changes. The fund uses a limited liability company structure to allow flexibility among different forms of support. The company plans to disclose more about its structure, governance, and valuation milestones over the coming months.

This provides a concrete corporate proposal for sharing the benefits AI creates. The shares' value will change as the company develops; future allocation and the groups prioritized still depend on the design to come. Within today's broader business theme, it extends the question from how companies operate to how their gains enter wider social life.

[Company announcement | Anthropic, October 18](https://www.anthropic.com/news/public-benefit-fund)

### Lindon Gao on bringing robots into complete workflows

Lindon Gao is co-founder and CEO of Dyna Robotics and previously founded smart-shopping-cart company Caper. His entrepreneurial experience spans retail operations and robotics products, with a focus on bringing technology into customers' actual businesses.

张小珺访谈录 is a long-form podcast about technology, business, and entrepreneurs. This October 15 interview discusses embodied-AI product approaches, deployment experience, and entrepreneurial choices.

In the published excerpts, Gao notes that even after signing paying customers, robotics companies can face extensive customization demands or fail to meet the required operating pace. He argues that products need to cover complete workflows rather than remain at the level of isolated actions. Folding, for example, is only part of a laundry's work: picking up, moving, and organizing items before and afterward also determine whether customers actually reduce their workload.

Read alongside the 格物通智 and 禾赛 news, this view connects different parts of embodied AI: perception, data, models, and applications ultimately need to work together at the customer's site. A robot successfully performing one action is readily visible to a buyer; how much of a work sequence it can take over is closer to its operational value.

### Hybrid Reasoning Agents explores more economical agent reasoning

Research follow-up; preprint dated October 13. The paper proposes switching agents between internal representations and textual interaction. Internal reasoning can use continuous vector representations; when tools, external feedback, or output are needed, the agent interacts with its environment through text. The authors design components connecting the two modes and use working memory to retain context across rounds of a task.

The problem is computational cost in long tasks. Conventional text-based reasoning generates substantial intermediate text. Performing part of the process in internal representations may reduce output tokens while retaining tool use. The authors report performance and efficiency improvements on several task benchmarks, within the models and test settings studied.

This research direction connects the cost of using agents with reasoning architecture. For application developers, it suggests that future efficiency gains may come from both model internals and external tool workflows. The paper remains a preprint; this issue provides a research overview and does not translate experimental gains directly into corporate bills.

[Paper abstract and version history](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.18861)

### web-edit adds copying to reduce friction across devices

Tool follow-up dated October 17. Simon Willison released web-edit 0.1a2, adding the ability to copy text and images to the clipboard. This small web application browses and edits folder contents. The maintainer's use case is everyday work: after remotely starting a task on another computer from a phone, users need a convenient way to retrieve generated content and logs.

Its value lies between workflow steps. Once a remote task finishes, the results still need to enter further organization, communication, or sharing. A convenient copy action reduces manual transfer. For people frequently switching between a phone and computer, such tools can improve the whole workflow. Deployment still requires appropriate access permissions.
