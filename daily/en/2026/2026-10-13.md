**Original title**: Microsoft to expand Azure Maia system to multi-gigawatt deployments

### Revisiting TokenRouter: how can systems avoid waiting when every step may switch models?

**Original title**: TokenRouter: Efficient Serving System for Token-Level LLM Routing

This October 8 preprint was discovered today and selected for further reading on the issue’s theme; it was not first published today. Token-level routing lets a single answer switch between models, but breaks single-model systems’ assumptions about synchronous batches. Fast models may wait for slow ones, and switched requests may wait for the next batch. The authors describe routing around requests, execute asynchronously around models, and use deferred batching.

Experiments used a server with eight A100-80G GPUs, comparing five routing algorithms on workloads including short reasoning, long reasoning, and multiround agent trajectories. In the checked Figure 1 example at concurrency eight, total throughput is 2.76 times the official R2R implementation, with ordinary engineering optimizations already contributing a 1.71-times result. Baseline choice therefore affects how gains are interpreted. These figures measure output-token throughput under specified conditions, not improved task accuracy or proportionally shorter waiting times for every user. The experiments were not reproduced, and the preprint is not presented as a peer-reviewed conclusion.

[Original](https://arxiv.org/html/2610.12242v1) · v1: October 8, 2026; further reading for this issue’s theme · Authors’ preprint; review scope: abstract, system design, experimental setup, and Figure 1; not reproduced.
