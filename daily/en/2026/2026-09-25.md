# Claude Tag Connects Personal Data; Gemini Live Adds an Avatar

From personal information in Slack to an avatar that can keep talking while looking things up, and faster local inference: this issue examines how new capabilities work and the conditions behind them. It translates the revised September 25 Chinese edition. The original 19-hour window runs from September 24 at 12:56 to September 25 at 07:56 Beijing time (UTC+8), or September 24 at 04:56–23:56 UTC; discovery looks back 72 hours.

## Top Stories

<!-- item:claude-tag -->
### Claude Tag brings personal connectors into channels; check who can see the answer

Claude Tag now supports personal connectors in Slack channels. Users can ask it to read documents, calendars or customer information they are authorized to access, then answer questions about work discussed in the channel.

Previously, Claude in channels mainly used shared tools connected by administrators. Personal work context can now enter the conversation: for example, comparing a plan in the user’s Drive with progress updates in the channel. **The capability is rolling out to Team plans, with Enterprise to follow.**

How the answer is shared matters. Users can review each response or choose an automatic mode in which Claude judges whether sensitive content needs review. **Once an answer is posted, every channel member can see it.** A file’s original access permissions do not mean the resulting response is restricted to the same readers.

**Condition:** personal connectors apply only to human-initiated requests. Scheduled tasks and actions Claude initiates on its own continue to use administrator-configured shared connectors.

<!-- item:gemini-avatar -->
### Gemini Live adds an avatar while continuing background tool use

Google’s Gemini 3.8 Live with Live Avatar adds synchronized facial expressions and lip movements to live voice conversations, while allowing dialogue to continue during background tool calls.

For a hotel check-in, a virtual receptionist could talk to the guest while retrieving information. For enterprise applications, the useful question is whether conversation, tool calls and business processes connect smoothly; the avatar is the visible part of that system.

The product is available in Gemini Enterprise. Preset avatars are offered; **custom avatars currently require enterprise allowlist eligibility.** Google says the generated audio and video carry SynthID watermarks.

<!-- item:dspark -->
### DSpark speeds up vision-language decoding; distinguish generation from total latency

Liquid AI released an experimental DSpark draft model for LFM2.5-VL-3B. A smaller model proposes candidate output for the target model to verify, reducing the wait for token-by-token generation.

The draft model has about **280 million parameters**, adding roughly 8.9% to the deployed parameter count. It accelerates decoding—the output-generation stage. Image encoding and input processing do not speed up by the same factor.

In the authors’ **M5 Max and MLX** tests, decoding was 2.30–3.13 times faster across tasks, while end-to-end speedups were 1.56–2.62 times. A maximum 3.13-fold decoding gain does not mean every request’s total wait falls to one-third.

**Condition:** these are author-reported, hardware- and task-dependent results. Running the model requires a DSpark-compatible build; an arbitrary installed version should not be assumed to support it.

<!-- item:protein -->
### Protein predictions covering over 2,800 viruses provide testable research leads

NVIDIA, Google DeepMind and EMBL-EBI released predictions of viral protein-complex structures covering more than 2,800 viruses, accessible through the AlphaFold database.

How proteins combine and interact helps explain how viruses work. The project combines AlphaFold 2 with NVIDIA BioNeMo computing workflows, giving researchers structural hypotheses from which to select interactions for further investigation.

**Prediction differs from experimental confirmation.** These are computational predictions with confidence information. They do not establish that every interaction has been verified experimentally or that a drug or vaccine has been obtained. Their immediate value is a larger set of research starting points to test.

Anthropic’s article explains context use in long coding sessions. The vendor reports about 40% lower billing cost than Opus 5 for typical workloads. ## Industry Insights

<!-- item:agent-governance -->
### When AI takes action, permissions and verification become part of the product

An OECD.AI article dated September 24 discusses interviews with 25 organizations in 11 countries. None reported deploying unrestricted autonomous agents. Practice concentrated on clear tasks with verifiable results and controllable or reversible error costs, keeping human confirmation at important steps.

This is a practitioner sample and cannot establish agent adoption across the whole industry.

**Our take:** alongside Claude Tag’s personal connectors and channel review, the product questions worth tracking are whose identity AI uses to obtain information, whom it acts for, who sees the result, and whether the process can be reconstructed after an error. These concrete designs affect whether a tool can enter everyday work.

## Research Briefs

<!-- item:trace-paper -->
### Can execution logs support audits if an agent can edit them?

*LLM Agents Can Easily Tamper With Their Own Traces* studies local coding-agent execution records. In the configurations tested by the authors, several frameworks let agents delete their records; external attacks could also exploit this gap.

The authors propose placing logging outside the agent’s control. For deployers, the lesson is to check not only whether records exist but whether the agent doing the work can alter or delete them. This issue reviewed the abstract and submission record, rather than reproducing the experiments.

<!-- item:speaker-memory -->
### SpeakerMem-R1: long-term memory also needs to remember who said it

Multi-person conversation errors can arise from attributing one person’s experience to someone else, as well as forgetting content. SpeakerMem-R1 stores speaker-tagged original statements alongside derived individual and group states, combining the evidence by person, event and time at retrieval.

The useful distinction is between retrieving relevant content and assigning it to the right person. Evaluation should separately examine content recall, speaker attribution and state updates, rather than treating one aggregate score as proof of reliable memory. Review was limited to the abstract and version record.

<!-- item:spatial-interactor -->
### Spatial-Interactor learns spatial changes before and after actions

Can a model update its understanding when an object moves or the viewpoint changes? Spatial-Interactor trains vision-language models with observation–action–next-observation trajectories, progressing from local changes to longer action sequences.

This shifts attention from static positional relations to continuous state updates. Spatial-reasoning tests must still be distinguished from complete robotics tasks. The paper was first submitted September 19 and discovered through the September 24 HF list; only the abstract and submission record were reviewed.

## Open Source Spotlight

<!-- item:ai-course -->
### AI Engineering from Scratch

**Purpose:** staged AI engineering learning material covering foundations, model applications and agent development, with an emphasis on reusable code or tools.

**Audience:** developers filling gaps along a learning path. Choose an entry point based on existing knowledge rather than reading everything from the beginning. The repository uses MIT.

<!-- item:hindsight -->
### Hindsight

**Purpose:** a service for writing, recalling and reflecting on agent memories, organized into separate memory banks for different people or projects.

**Before integrating:** inspect isolation, links back to original material, deletion and updates. These details affect the cost of correcting long-term memory. The repository uses MIT.

<!-- item:univer -->
### Univer

**Purpose:** embed spreadsheets, documents and slides in a product through plugins and APIs. It is relevant to teams turning AI output into editable office content.

**Before integrating:** the open SDK uses Apache-2.0. Optional Univer Pro features have separate product and licensing boundaries; not every commercial feature is open source.

<!-- item:google-ax -->
### Google AX

**Purpose:** declarative configuration for agent tasks, workspaces and models, with sandbox execution and cluster orchestration capabilities.

**Before integrating:** the README warns that the project is unstable and may introduce incompatible changes. It is better suited to architecture study or experiments in an isolated environment. The repository uses Apache-2.0.
