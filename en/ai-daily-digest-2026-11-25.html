Industry and investment

Anthropic closes below its $83 offer price less than a week after listing

Anthropic shares finished the November 24 U.S. trading session below their $83 initial public offering price, according to the Associated Press’s market close report. Earlier in the day, TechCrunch reported that the shares had traded as low as about $76 by midday. That figure was an intraday price.

According to the company’s offering announcement, Anthropic’s Class A common stock began trading on Nasdaq on November 19 under the ticker ANT. The offer price was $83 per share. Anthropic sold newly issued shares, and some existing shareholders sold a portion of their holdings. Once the stock began trading, investors could buy and sell at different prices. Those trades do not change the proceeds already paid to the sellers in the offering.

AI companies preparing to go public now have a fresh price to compare themselves with each trading day. The share price alone tells us little about changes in customer orders or how the company’s models compare with competitors’.

Two amateur astronomers use AI to flag possible planets for follow-up

OpenAI describes two independent searches. Engineer Kevin Bond used Codex to write and refine a program that looked for recurring dips in brightness in public Kepler telescope observations of roughly 100,000 stars. The program recovered signals from some previously confirmed planets. In a separate search, an astronomer at the SETI Institute found two of the same candidate signals Bond had flagged. One star dimmed about every 13.8 days and the other about every 4.8 days. Astronomers can also look for slight motion of a star along our line of sight, another possible sign of a planet’s gravitational pull.

Ethan Byrd used Astra to design searches and examine observations of roughly 19 million stars. One search looked broadly for planet candidates, while the other focused on the habitable zones around stars. Byrd has sent one candidate to researchers working on NASA’s Transiting Exoplanet Survey Satellite mission for further analysis. Stars eclipsing each other and temperature changes in a telescope’s instruments can also cause an apparent dip in brightness. Further observations can test whether a dip returns on schedule and whether other measurements point to the same planet.

Music platforms

Tidal will reject and remove fully AI-generated recordings from December 9

Tidal says on its support page that it will reject and remove recordings generated entirely by AI beginning December 9. Its current approach includes labeling those recordings and excluding them from royalty payments. Tidal told TechCrunch that songs whose creators use AI tools in writing or production can still be accepted. The policy applies to the finished recording. A listener cannot reconstruct the whole production process from a melody alone.

Tidal says it identified more than 40% of the tracks submitted to it over the past 90 days as AI-generated. That share describes recent submissions. It does not establish what proportion of the entire catalog was AI-generated. Musicians who believe a recording was flagged in error can submit supporting material and request a review through Tidal’s support page.

Agent evaluation

ShellBench puts old emails and receipts in the same test. The leading model finished about six in ten tasks

The OpenClaw Foundation and its collaborators released ShellBench to test AI assistants in simulated workspaces containing calendars, receipts, spreadsheets and email. They drew task patterns from anonymized usage records and tested 12 models on 100 tasks across five operating environments. Most tasks had a scoring rubric, and every required criterion had to be met for the task to count as complete.

Averaged across environments, the top-ranked model completed 62.5% of the tasks. A workspace could contain outdated records or accounts with similar names. Many tasks required the assistant to identify the document that resolved a discrepancy. If a later email corrected an amount, for example, the assistant had to find it and show the person taking over where the correction came from. The score reflects these particular tasks and grading rules. The team released its methods and a breakdown of the results, with the full paper still to come.

Pinterest announced its redesigned parental controls page on November 23. TechCrunch reported on the AI recommendation setting the following day. Parents can adjust how much AI-generated content appears in a teen’s recommendations, as well as manage notifications and video autoplay separately. iFood gives coding agents a work order that engineers can check

In a customer account published by OpenAI, Brazilian food delivery company iFood describes bringing maintenance jobs once run separately by different teams onto an internal platform covering more than 15,000 code repositories. Jobs can run in the background. At the end, Structured Outputs returns the result in predefined fields that can also record a failure. iFood has used the platform to check for dependency problems and attempt repairs. A consistent format lets the next engineer see which jobs are complete and which still need attention.

01When does a preference become a decision? Researchers examine 198 papers on human–AI co-creation
ResearchVersion 1 submitted November 23 and listed by arXiv on November 24Table 1, the classification of commitment and the discussion in version 1 analyze system descriptions in 198 papers. The authors did not run a comparative user trial.

Ashwin Ram and colleagues analyzed 198 papers from 11 SIGCHI conferences and proposed six dimensions for understanding intent. One concerns how firmly someone has committed to a choice. A person may know exactly what they want to see and still be deciding whether to use it. Most of the systems the authors examined had no separate way to express that distinction, and few tracked who first suggested an idea. Asking to preview a poster with a blue background and approving blue for an entire print run mention the same color but call for different actions.

The authors classified designs described in the literature. The 198 papers represent neither 198 groups testing poster tools nor scores for a particular product. Version 1 of the preprint was submitted on November 23 and appeared in arXiv’s public human–computer interaction listing on November 24.

1
llama.cpp keeps the old prompt-cache entry until the entire restore succeedsMIT

Release b12675 of llama.cpp, a project for running models locally, changes when its server clears an entry while restoring the prompt cache. The merged change describes a case in which the main restore succeeds but an auxiliary step fails. Clearing data too early could leave the old entry incomplete. The server now retains it until the whole restore succeeds.

The cache saves computation already done on the first part of an input so that a later request can reuse it. Keeping the entry intact leaves the server with a state it can retry if restoration fails. The project uses the MIT license. This item draws on the maintainers’ November 24 release record. Speed and recovery behavior were not benchmarked for this issue.
