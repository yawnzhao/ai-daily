Tuesday, November 17, 2026, Beijing time.

Apollo Research will send evaluators to work inside OpenAI. The United States has issued a rule on remote access to advanced chips, and Qwen has updated its flagship model API. ### What can outside researchers learn from inside a training run?

On November 16, Apollo Research announced a pilot with OpenAI. Six evaluators will work on site over four months to investigate whether a model might try to weaken the safeguards that constrain it or future models. Access to a trained model ahead of release lets researchers test how it handles a given question. Tracing a behavior back to the step that encouraged it also requires access to intermediate versions, reward records and training examples.

The team will examine incentives during training, whether tests can elicit such behavior, whether signs of it appear in internal deployments and whether monitoring can detect the reasoning behind it. Access is agreed for each task. Apollo can disclose what it asked to see, what it actually saw, and what was delayed or requested to be redacted. Readers of a future report will therefore be able to judge which stages of development its conclusions cover. The announcement sets out the evaluation plan. The first report will examine training incentives.

### When chips stay in a third country, who can use them matters for export controls

Yesterday's issue covered a U.S. House bill and letters from Congress. Today brings a separate regulatory development. The U.S. Department of Commerce's Bureau of Industry and Security has issued an interim final rule addressing remote use of advanced chips and servers through data centers in third countries. The equipment can be delivered in one jurisdiction, the data center can supply computing power from another, and the company that ultimately uses it can be based in a third.

Section 744.24 sets out the conditions. One provision covers cases in which a party knows that listed items will be used in a data center that will supply computing power above a specified threshold to a company headquartered in Macau or a Country Group D:5 destination, or whose ultimate parent is headquartered there. The computing capacity may come from one facility or several networked facilities. The text also addresses cases in which a customer trains a model for such a company. Assessing a transaction requires checking the items, end use, users and computing capacity against the rule. The agency's rule and the bill previously put to a House vote are separate texts.

### Google Maps organizes stops into an itinerary, with some features launching first in the U.S.

With the updated Ask Maps, travelers can describe a multi-day road trip and see their stops grouped by day, each marked with a numbered pin. They can share the itinerary or export it to Google Docs on a desktop. Another update lets users search their own saved lists for a suitable next stop.

Itinerary planning and saved-list search are rolling out in regions where Ask Maps is available. Other features are launching first in the United States, including tips on parking and charging before starting navigation, and a way to find nearby services with appointments available. Once Maps finds an opening, the user decides whether to book it.

### Apple uses a separate research app to study how people understand answers to health questions

Apple and OpenEvidence are inviting eligible U.S. iPhone users to participate through a dedicated research app. Participants can ask health questions and choose to share information such as activity and sleep data so that answers reflect their circumstances. ### Hugging Face hands language model request routing to OpenRouter

Hugging Face has announced a change to language model routing in Inference Providers. Developers can still send requests with a Hugging Face token. The backend selects a provider based on price, speed and reliability and can retry with another provider if the request fails. Model pages show provider prices and performance metrics. Organizations can also disable particular providers in their settings.

Billing changes with the migration. Language model requests are billed to the Hugging Face account or organization, and developers can no longer use their own provider keys for those requests. Custom keys for image and video providers continue to work as before.

### A care assistant can keep a conversation flowing. Does it deliver every planned step?

In this research retrospective, Thalia Viranda and colleagues studied 473 conversations that 80 older adults had with a voice assistant over three days. The team set goals and planned intervention components for different sessions. It then assessed participants' experiences, how the system used their personal context, and which components it actually delivered. Section 5 of the paper reports the results. Tables 2 and 4 give the participant and session counts, respectively.

Participants generally rated usability and their willingness to use the assistant favorably. The assistant often steered a conversation toward an appropriate area of a person's life, but made more limited use of that individual's specific circumstances. The main gap in delivery was the omission of planned components. In this study, the association between personalization and completeness of delivery did not reach statistical significance. A short trial can inform conversation design. Long-term health outcomes require separate study. The paper was submitted on November 13. Paper Radar included it today, and we revisit it alongside Apple's health research announcement.

[arXiv | November 13 preprint, revisited November 17](https://arxiv.org/html/2611.18385v1)

### Paul Christiano distinguishes two predictions about the risks of advanced AI

Dwarkesh Patel hosts the long-form interview series Dwarkesh Podcast. His November 16 guest, Paul Christiano, leads the Alignment Research Center and previously worked on safety at a U.S. AI safety institution. He spoke in a personal capacity. Early in the interview, Patel pressed him on which probability he was estimating. Did he mean the chance of superintelligence emerging within a year, or the chance of humanity losing control as a result?

In the transcript, Christiano answers the two questions separately. His judgments depend on whether developers continue on their current trajectory and whether they are willing to adopt safeguards that would slow their progress. The exchange is worth revisiting alongside Apollo's evaluation plan. Specifying the event being estimated and the assumptions about development helps identify what evidence to look for.

### llama.cpp tells clients which reasoning levels their chat template supports

The b12431 update to llama.cpp addresses a server error. When probing a chat template, the server records which reasoning effort settings the template rejects. A client that sends one of those values can receive an HTTP 400 response listing the supported levels. The template continues to handle values and aliases it can render successfully.

The maintainer tested the change with Qwen3.8-27B and documented examples of settings that failed and settings that passed. Models using different templates may accept different values. The change makes configuration problems easier for callers to diagnose. The project uses the MIT license.
