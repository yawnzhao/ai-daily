Capital markets

Anthropic lists on Nasdaq, with its founders retaining a say in control

Anthropic began trading on Nasdaq on November 19, US time, under the ticker ANT. The Verge reports that the $83 offer price valued the company at about $1.5 trillion. The shares opened at $111.50. According to WIRED, the IPO raised about $81 billion, or more than $107 billion including privately sold shares. The stock gained over 30% on its first day. Both publications described it as the largest IPO on record.

The prospectus put first-half revenue at $16.3 billion and the net loss at $80.7 billion. Most of the loss came from an accounting charge that did not reflect operating performance. WIRED also reported commitments to pay suppliers more than $900 billion over the next 19 years, primarily to build computing capacity. A residents’ group in West Virginia is opposing a data center being built for Anthropic.

As a public benefit corporation, Anthropic’s board must weigh its mission alongside shareholder returns. The Long-Term Benefit Trust holds a special class of shares that lets it appoint four of seven directors, although one appointment effectively needs the founders’ assent, according to The Verge. At Nasdaq, CEO Dario Amodei said the company would pace model training and deployment until it was confident the systems were safe and under human control, even under commercial or political pressure. Future decisions will show how that commitment holds up against quarterly earnings expectations.

Browser safety

Google builds a mirror internet to test Chrome agents

WIRED reports that Google’s Chrome team has publicly described Mirror Dimension for the first time. It is an isolated web environment under Google’s control, with realistic social networks, shops and banking back offices. Agents perform tasks in a regular Chrome browser. Hidden prompt-injection attempts are part of the evaluation of their defenses.

The team says small test environments offer too little exposure to realistic pages. Models may also recognize tests and behave differently, while testing on the open internet creates risks. Chrome AI security engineer Charles Qian says it is impossible to enumerate everything an agent might encounter on a hostile web. The team observed agents learning which information a task requires and when to ask the user for confirmation. Site-building agents working within a dedicated framework populated the test sites with details such as coupon codes and social profiles.

The researchers controlled the sites’ certificate and verification infrastructure so that a regular browser could operate inside the simulation. Google emphasized that this depended on control of the entire closed network. Engineering director Doug Turner said the team shared the approach to encourage consideration by other teams inside and outside Google. Google plans to make the environment available to more of its own teams.

Agents

Gemini can operate a Mac, with users choosing the apps it may access

Google has added computer use to the Gemini app for macOS, The Verge reports. It can carry out multistep tasks across local files, desktop apps and the web. Google’s examples include filling in forms, making presentations and posting a secondhand item for sale.

Users choose which apps Gemini may access. It works in a background window while the chat shows a live preview, so users can follow along. The feature is rolling out in English to US users.

The report comes as Apple prepares to tighten the rules for apps requesting Full Disk Access on the Mac, partly because of risks introduced by agents that operate computers. As agents take on more tasks, deciding where permission begins and ends becomes a product-design question.

Education policy

US school districts consider alternatives to Google Search over AI controls

Public school systems in New York, Los Angeles and Chicago are considering search providers other than Google, according to reporting cited by The Verge. Google had said that from next year schools disabling its Gemini-powered AI features would also lose access to Search. Chris Phillips, a Google education vice president, said search and AI were now deeply integrated.

New York Mayor Zohran Mamdani, Senator Bernie Sanders and others characterized the arrangement as extortion. New York City schools chancellor Kamar H. Samuels said the district would look for alternatives if Google maintained its position. Chicago Public Schools also told Google it would seek another option unless it could get a version of Search without AI.

Google spokesperson Laura Breen said the company understood concerns about students using AI to take shortcuts. Starting this month, Google will pilot guided learning in Search to take students through problems step by step. It will also change school administration settings next year so that the Gemini app, Gemini Notebook and Search default to off and schools choose what to enable. Many US classrooms rely heavily on Google Classroom and Chromebooks, making a complete switch difficult.

04
The Gemini API loads tool parameters on demand

Google introduced Tool Retrieval in preview in the Gemini Interactions API. Sending full parameter descriptions for dozens or hundreds of tools with every request adds input tokens and latency and can make tool selection less accurate. The model normally sees tool names and descriptions, then requests a tool by name when needed. The API loads the parameters and calls the tool within the same request. Developers add the tool_search tool and set defer_loading to true. The feature supports Gemini 3.6 and later models.

01Which agent failures do people struggle to notice or recall?
ResearchOctober 26 preprint, revisited from Paper-RadarFull preprint, including sections 3, 4.1.4, 4.2 and 6.3 and Figure 4, on the corpus, interviews, comparisons and limitations.

As agents take on more desktop and web tasks, an October paper from Microsoft and Georgia Tech researchers examines delegation from the user’s perspective. Which failures recur in multistep tasks, and which are easy to miss?

The authors analyzed 163,487 Reddit posts describing experiences with AI agents between January 2025 and June 2026. They identified 23 types of delegation failure in seven groups. Examples include ignoring instructions, spinning without making progress, and excessive flattery or apologies. Section 3 reports that the share of complaints about ignoring instructions fell over those 18 months while the share about agents spinning without stopping rose. The researchers then asked 73 experienced agent users to rate the frequency, severity and detectability of each type of failure.

Section 4.2 reports that only 36 participants could recall a specific failure without prompting. Their accounts covered 14 of the 23 types. When shown concrete scenarios, at least one participant recognized each type from experience. Although 81% had encountered excessive agreeableness, nobody mentioned it during free recall. Failures rated more severe also tended to be rated harder to detect.

Sections 3 and 6.3 describe the limitations. Reddit contributors selected themselves, coding communities supplied many of the posts, and participants tended to be young and highly educated. The taxonomy is not exhaustive. The authors propose making delegation easier to supervise and studying common but underexamined problems such as spinning.
