### Claude renames its expensive tiers Max and Ultra, and verified Ultra accounts lose the five-hour cap

On October 31, Anthropic adjusted Claude's two expensive subscriptions. The former Max 5x is renamed Max and stays 100 dollars a month. The former Max 20x is renamed Ultra and stays 200 dollars a month. Existing subscriptions move over automatically. The change that matters is Ultra: a verified Ultra account is no longer bound by a rolling five-hour window and can be used continuously inside the weekly allowance. The weekly allowance is unchanged and is still 10 times Pro. Qbitai and 36Kr, relaying the official explanation, say most existing Ultra subscribers start receiving the change the same day. Newer accounts keep a temporary limit until automatic verification using account and payment history, and they get an email when it lifts. The 100-dollar Max still keeps the five-hour cap. Ultra also supports Claude Code.

The Claude pricing page says Max usage every five hours and every week is 5 times Pro, and Ultra is 10 times Pro's weekly usage with no five-hour limit on a verified account. Both tiers can put up to half the weekly allowance toward Fable, and both include a monthly API credit: 100 dollars for Max and 200 dollars for Ultra. Qbitai reports that Mike Overell, who leads Claude subscription products, explained that the old "20 times" referred only to a single five-hour window, while the weekly allowance was actually 10 times Pro. He acknowledged the naming was easy to misunderstand and apologized. The day before, OpenAI had just doubled ChatGPT Pro usage. The two companies' contest over subscription allowances is still going.

### OpenAI is reported to have cut its new fundraising target; NVIDIA is considering a debt guarantee for Nscale's Anthropic data center

TechCrunch on October 31 gathered several news reports. OpenAI had been reported as planning to raise 30 billion dollars at a 1.4 trillion dollar valuation, and later the valuation was said to have fallen to 1.25 trillion dollars. The Financial Times reported on Friday that the company has cut the fundraising target to 20 billion dollars, and the first closing is expected to be lower still. The round is led by investors from the United Arab Emirates, and a later offering aimed at wealthy individuals could push the total to 20 billion dollars. A follow-up in the Wall Street Journal says OpenAI has so far secured 11 billion dollars, and some large investors have been cool. TechCrunch thinks part of the reason is timing: Wall Street's wariness of heavy AI-infrastructure spending has already set off a tech selloff. OpenAI has not responded.

The same pressure reaches data-center finance. TechCrunch, citing The Information, says NVIDIA is in talks to guarantee billions of dollars of debt for the AI cloud provider Nscale, for a data center planned for Anthropic in West Virginia. Anthropic has signed a 44.6 billion dollar contract for the campus's first 460-megawatt building, expected to use NVIDIA's next-generation Vera Rubin chips. Under a residual-value guarantee, if Nscale defaults on construction payments, NVIDIA would step in, temporarily take over the facility, and help find a buyer and a new tenant. NVIDIA's earlier support for cloud providers was mostly leases and chip purchases, rarely construction. The report notes that as of last week, CoreWeave, Nebius, and IREN were all down more than 30 percent since the start of October.

### An AI agent runs a bench and completes a brain injection in a live mouse; one model refuses before the needle goes in

WIRED reported on October 31 that Gabriel Ocana Santero, chief executive of the San Francisco biotech Exin Therapeutics, let an AI agent directly operate a custom surgical bench and complete a brain injection in a live mouse with epilepsy, testing a gene therapy that might reduce seizures. The bench includes a motorized device that holds the mouse, a camera for the agent, an injection needle, and a dosing device. A veterinary surgeon first opened the skull. The agent then chose one of 19 therapies from a table, set the brain region and injection coordinates, and controlled the arm, the needle, and the volume and speed of the injection.

The team first tried Anthropic's Claude Opus 5.5. It finished the experimental plan but refused before the needle went in, on the ground that it might stop midway and endanger the animal. The team continued only after switching to Claude Sonnet 5. The setup required a human to approve the injection plan, had an emergency button that could cut the motors, and forbade the agent from changing the device's low-level code. During the procedure the device coordinate system failed. The agent recognized that the calculated position was wrong, but refused to change the code because the instruction forbade it. The team used a separate agent running Opus 5 to change the code, and did not tell it a live surgery was underway. The operation took a bit more than the usual 45 minutes, a little over an hour. The mouse recovered well. Whether the therapy works is still to be seen. Santero said human supervision is essential when a live animal is involved. Anthropic did not comment. The above is the company's own account and has not been independently reviewed.

[WIRED | October 31, 2026](https://www.wired.com/story/an-ai-agent-did-brain-surgery-on-live-mouse/)

### Apple Intelligence opens for testing in China on November 4, with part of the experience connected to Alibaba's Qwen

On October 31, Apple announced that the first Apple Intelligence features are coming to China. They include live translation in Messages, FaceTime, and Phone; writing tools for rewriting, proofreading, and summarizing; visual intelligence; Clean Up in Photos; Genmoji; priority messages in Mail; and transcription and summaries of Phone and Notes recordings. Apple is explicit that this update does not include Siri AI or the other next-generation Apple Intelligence features announced at WWDC 2026. ### A WIRED check of about 50 companies: actual orders from shopping agents such as Meta's Muse are still few

WIRED on October 31 went through earnings calls and interviews at about 50 companies in travel, telecom, restaurants, and warehousing. Meta says its agent Muse, which can finish online tasks automatically, is becoming an important force in shopping, that millions of people use it to place orders, and that the average amount is more than 200 dollars. Zuckerberg said on this month's earnings call that more than 10 million people in the United States and Canada have turned Muse on, and more than 25 percent of them use it to shop, above the company's expectation. Meta says it is working with 10 retailers, including Walmart, Best Buy, and Gap.

Most executives have not seen an impact in front of them. DoorDash said orders from agents such as Muse are only a few hundred. T-Mobile said it has not seen any significant volume. Hilton called agent bookings negligible. Ally Financial and eBay also said the scale is small. The fitness chain Life Time said not one membership cancellation came from an agent. Many executives instead think that if agents really spread, they would benefit more. WIRED notes that several of these retail partners had already reported earnings before Muse launched and have no data yet. Agent shopping is still early.

[WIRED | October 31, 2026](https://www.wired.com/story/metas-muse-could-upend-all-kinds-of-businesses-it-hasnt-yet/)

### A personal experiment: GPT-5.6 Sol, in Codex, finishes a peaceful game of Factorio on its own

Developer Ryan Madden wrote on his blog that he had GPT-5.6 Sol, running in Codex at a low thinking setting, finish a peaceful game of Factorio, with no hostile creatures, in 13.5 hours of in-game time. The model operated the game through a small Python library that enforces the same limits as a human player, such as reach and the fog of war. Madden said that late in the game the model failed to connect some production systems and had to carry materials by hand, over and over, to launch the rocket. An earlier GPT-5.5 could not even use a conveyor belt. This is a single personal experiment.

### DeepSeek open-sources the research prototype dsh-superagent: a team coding agent that acts only when asked

DeepSeek published dsh-superagent-prototype on GitHub and noted that it is a research prototype for reference, not an official product. The code comes from the team's daily development. A large amount of the engineering was done by coding agents, and it is not the same as the internal version. It is a proactive coding agent for a single GitHub repository. It listens to GitHub and can optionally connect to Feishu. After an approved member states a need, it opens a work item, finishes it in a separate session and directory, and replies in the original conversation, for example by opening a pull request. A web panel shows what a person still has to handle. The design principle is to take as little of a person's attention as possible: act only when asked or when team rules allow, and speak only when a person must act or can use the result. The repository was not run. The license was not checked.

### A related paper: a 9A framework for human-AI research, and who is authorized to make the critical decision

Why read it today: in the mouse experiment above, the agent chooses the plan and operates the equipment, the person keeps approval and the emergency stop, and the model's own refusal also changes the experiment. This preprint, submitted to arXiv on October 16, 2026, treats human-AI collaborative research as a systems-design problem and proposes a 9A framework. It is not a new paper from today. The organization layer asks which research activities are needed, who carries them out, and who is authorized for the critical decisions. The coordination layer asks how participants get information, how work moves through proposal, execution, checking, revision, and adjudication, and when the collaboration arrangement should change. The evaluation layer defines what counts as effective performance, how to design controls that identify the effect of those organizational and coordination choices, and what evidence supports a conclusion. The reading scope is the abstract. The paper has not been peer reviewed. It was selected from the Paper-Radar library.

### Nenad Tomašev: a hypothesis from AI still has to go back to the laboratory

Nenad Tomašev is a researcher at Google DeepMind. This interview is from Machine Learning Street Talk. The Concourse record dates the episode to October 20, 2026. What follows is an editorial paraphrase the Concourse page arranged from automatic-caption segments, not a verbatim quotation.

He thinks that in sciences that are not purely computational, a hypothesis generated by AI still needs a physical experiment to test it. Investing in models should come with investment in laboratories, experts, and researchers. He also suggests that, in a suitable workflow, AI can be switched off for a time so a person judges independently, to reduce automation bias and keep the person's ability to think. This is a view from about ten days ago, not a response to today's mouse experiment. It does point at the work a person still has to do once an agent walks onto the bench.
