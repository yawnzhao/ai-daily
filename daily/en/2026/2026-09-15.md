### Our analysis: authority and investment reshape the debate

Trump’s rejection, Johnson’s tentative meeting and corporate-first proposals, and Charles’s neutral venue move agenda-setting beyond lab statements. House recess and party disagreement constrain federal action; the Anthropic/Pentagon cases remain in court. The archive’s prediction of little pre-election legislative progress is editorial, not certainty.

NVIDIA’s $12.9303 billion announcement and ByteDance’s $29.6 billion proposed loan predate Amodei’s September 12 appeal. ByteDance’s up-to-$70 billion spending is a contemplated ceiling, not realized expenditure. Together with Anthropic’s reported profits and Z.ai’s sell-off, they illustrate ongoing investment beside louder restraint proposals.

The issue sees a second fault line between openness and coordinated pacing: developers fear restricted access, while the largest model community is associated with a chip supplier opposed to slowing. NVIDIA’s openness promises depend on future practice; independent assessment remains an open question. A principles charter might offer shared language, but neither a neutral venue nor a statement creates an enforceable rule. Commercial incentives and political vetoes may matter more than declarations alone.

### Expert regrading changes reported physics-benchmark results

Experts reassess six major physics benchmarks, identifying ambiguous questions, incorrect references and unclear grading criteria among apparent model errors. GPT-5.6-Sol’s HLE-Physics mean@4 rises from 47.3% to 78.7%, with several leading benchmarks approaching saturation.

The study suggests some evaluations underestimate frontier capability. This complements the Fields medalists’ question about mathematical goals: both the objective and the measuring instrument warrant scrutiny. Results are the paper’s reported regrading, not independent digest replication.

[Reference 1](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.13009)
