NVIDIA reinstates Jensen Huang while independent directors continue their investigation

NVIDIA announced on November 23 that its board had voted unanimously to reinstate Jensen Huang as president, CEO and a director, effective immediately. Harvey C. Jones will remain board chair. Colette Kress, who served as interim CEO, continues as chief financial officer.

The board said it met with Huang several times and heard from senior U.S. government officials. On the strength of that input, the board concluded that reinstating him posed no national security risk. The same announcement said a committee of independent directors had retained outside counsel and would continue its investigation. Separately, the U.S. Senate Banking Committee lists Huang as a witness for its December 3 public hearing on export controls. The board’s decision took effect immediately. The hearing is still to come.

Google Search can read your wallet. What can it do with that access?

Google has begun rolling out an option to connect Google Wallet to AI Mode in Search. People who opt in can ask Search to organize their spending or compare cash-back and travel benefits across linked cards. Google says the connection is read-only. It gives Search no authority to move, manage or invest the user’s money. The Wallet rollout began November 23. Connections to Google Drive files and the Gemini app are due in the following weeks.

Google gives tax preparation as one example for the file connection. Someone with records scattered across documents, spreadsheets and PDFs could use them to draw up a plan. Users can turn each app connection on or off individually. Google also plans to let them see what context went into a personalized answer. That gives users three separate questions to ask. What have I searched for before? Which records have I let Search read this time? What can it actually do on my behalf?

Anthropic separates code review, fixes and approval

Anthropic introduced Claude Ship in beta for Team and Enterprise customers with repositories on GitHub. Code Review, already generally available, flags problems. The new PR Steward handles failed checks, merge conflicts and review comments, explains what it changed and shows how to undo the change. Approval and merging sit outside its role. Another beta feature, Claude Approvals, uses rules a team writes into its repository to decide which routine changes can proceed and which need a person to sign off.

Administrators can start in observation mode on one repository and compare the system’s judgments with actual reviews before making approval a condition for a change to proceed. Existing branch protections and required checks still apply. Anthropic illustrated the division of work with a small example from its own documentation. A passage about which usage data an administrator key could read was easy to misinterpret. The team corrected it, and a documentation reviewer took part in signing off. Even a change that small has a writer, someone to fix it and someone responsible for approval.

Computing infrastructure

Amazon agrees to buy Decart technology that helps models run more efficiently on chips

Amazon has signed an agreement to buy the proprietary technology behind the Decart Optimization Stack and plans to bring Decart’s co-founders and research and development team on board. The companies have worked together on Amazon’s Trainium chips since 2025. Amazon says it wants to apply that experience tuning software and hardware together to its own chips and models, so customers can train and run models faster and at lower cost.

The companies have signed the agreement. Closing still depends on conditions including the required regulatory approvals. Amazon gave no single figure customers could use to calculate the speedup for their own workloads. A chip’s arithmetic capacity tells you little on its own about how long a particular model will take to run.

Z.ai’s GLM-5.4 sends information through the same layers more than once

In a November 23 technical note, Z.ai described GLM-5.4. The team continued training a pretrained model so that, during one forward pass, a set of layers with shared weights can run several times. Each round works on the internal state left by the last. That gives the model more computational depth, and each trip through the layers costs compute. Counting parameters alone misses how much processing each token receives.

Z.ai listed ways to use the model, including its coding plans. It described API access as forthcoming and said it would release the weights after completing the necessary safety work. The team is still exploring how to give harder tokens more rounds of processing.

01If an assistant forgets your preference, will it still plan around it?
ResearchVersion 1 submitted November 20 and listed by arXiv on November 23Sections 7, 9 and 10 of version 1 cover the scenarios, limitations and conclusions.

A preprint by Zibin Lin and colleagues follows one preference through three scenarios involving scheduling, learning from feedback and withdrawing information. A user is unavailable in the evenings. If the assistant stops reading that preference, will the habits it learned still influence its bookings? The authors examine the original memory separately from the state derived from it. An assistant might stop repeating what the user said and still act on it later.

The paper is a thematic review that uses examples and controlled diagnostics to illustrate how to tell the difference. In Section 9 the authors explain that the three scenarios were never run in sequence on a single complete product. The paper did not test Google Wallet, Search or Lingguang. The authors submitted version 1 on November 20, and arXiv listed it on November 23. We read Sections 7, 9 and 10 of the full paper for this issue.
