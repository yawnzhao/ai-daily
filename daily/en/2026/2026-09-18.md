### Anthropic publishes three measures of internal AI R&D pace

The September 17 official account uses Epoch AI’s AL0–AL5 scale. As of August 2026, no measured subset runs fully autonomously, while Claude leads 26% of internal AI R&D at AL4—most work end to end from high-level instructions under human supervision—and collaborates or more in over 90%.

Its most-used internal platform has about 30,000 agents doing research/engineering at a time, constrained by online interception and offline review. Reporting dimensions include coverage, review delay and escalation rate.

A July 13–20 snapshot assigns about 6% of AI R&D compute to safety and about 12% within AI-driven AI R&D. Anthropic calls these conservative and excludes another substantial safety-classifier allocation. It proposes independently verifiable industry metrics and embedded third parties with access comparable to internal risk teams to check practices, incidents and metrics. These are self-measurements without a unified method; they are not already independently validated. If coordination changes pace, the company expects these measures to change.

[Reference 1](https://www.anthropic.com/institute/measuring-pace-of-ai-development)

### OpenAI announces Astra for Law

The September 17 official post describes GPT-6 Astra adapted for legal research, indexing over 230 million URLs through Free Law Project/CourtListener and covering over 99.9% of published US precedential case law. On Vals AI’s 200-question legal-research benchmark, reported accuracy is 54.0% versus 38.7% for the same model with web search, approximately 40% relative improvement.

OpenAI reports 24% more cited cases on case-law questions and up to 54% more relevant passages from correct opinions at equal reasoning effort; its example comparison criticizes a Fable 5.1 citation to an overturned case. These are vendor evaluations, not replicated findings.

Selected firms receive Trusted Access in ChatGPT/Codex, with API name gpt-6-astra-law; Harvey and Legora plan integrations. Eligible firms receive API zero-data-retention terms and default exclusion from human review in ChatGPT Enterprise. Latham & Watkins helps design information barriers. Twenty-six legal plugins include iManage, Intapp and HighQ; Sullivan & Cromwell is among three custom builders. The issue interprets specialized retrieval and governance as packaged trust infrastructure.

### Our analysis: elevation, measurement and expansion coexist

Charles’s summit elevates control to a head-of-state forum; possible September 24 meetings connect the debate with geopolitics. Anthropic’s 26%, over-90%, 30,000-agent and 6% safety-compute measurements give concrete indicators without answering what pace is acceptable. Independent access is required before treating self-reports as externally verified measures.

Huang’s unspecified chip forecast, Huawei’s future roadmap, specialized legal tools and sponsored-agent tests show continued plans for supply and commercialization. Amazon emphasizes testing, and energy-management participants emphasize flexible grid connections. The issue interprets these as alternative responses to risk and resource constraints, not a shared agreement to slow.

Next signals: whether the White House meeting occurs, whether other labs publish comparable metrics, and whether proposed external-evaluation obligations require their verification. Discussion can rise in prominence while expansion continues; these separate observations should not be merged into causal claims.

### How Model Growth, Recursion, and Boundary Operators Influence Scaling Exponents

The 44-page, code-released study examines dynamically increasing recurrent depth and boundary operators that normalize and inject earlier-block information. Authors report architectural interventions changing pretraining scaling exponents, with smaller gains from boundary operators in ordinary transformers.

A 7.4B growth architecture matches GPT-3 13B on CORE with about twentyfold less compute, with gains increasing at scale. In their data-limited multi-epoch setting, growing recurrent depth with scale is compute-optimal. These are reported experiments, not a universal twentyfold saving.

[Reference 1](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.19107)

<!-- item:papers-02 -->
### Label-free Steering adapts about 100,000 bias parameters

Majority-vote pseudo-label rewards and a frozen backbone reduce optimized parameters by 76,000 times versus full-parameter TTRL. Reported MATH-500 is 76.67%, slightly above the authors’ supervised-label reproduction.

The procedure also improves MathVista, AI2D, LogicVista and MMAU; steering vectors transfer to 4,500 held-out MATH questions. Parameter reduction is not an identical reduction in total cost or a guarantee that every deployed model learns safely during use.

[Reference 1](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.18587)

<!-- item:papers-03 -->
### One Axis, No Brake studies multi-agent correction limits

Selecting helpful revisions while rejecting harmful ones is related to detecting model self-knowledge, whose AUROC across six families ranges 0.64–0.89. White-box steering along correctness directions moves beneficial and harmful revisions together in tested conditions.

When most agents begin wrong, debate can amplify shared mistakes into confident consensus; more agents, diversity or stronger members do not rescue the reported settings. Adding information before revision helps more than filtering after. The abstract page displays July 17, 2026 submission despite first appearance in the September 17 list; that discrepancy is retained rather than silently redated.

[Reference 1](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.18998)

<!-- item:papers-04 -->
### Capability Emergence Can Be Forecast examines induction heads

Previous-token-head formation predicts induction-head emergence across seeds with Spearman ρ=0.977 and median lead of 975 steps, roughly 15% of training. Conformal intervals cover 15/15 held-out seeds; frozen rules pass preregistered gates in three unseen configurations. Eighty anchored runs yield t_event ≈ 1.19 × t_anchor.

This is a forecast of the studied mechanism, not all emergent abilities. The abstract page displays July 17, 2026 submission; first discovery in the September 17 list remains distinct.

[Reference 1](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.19000)
