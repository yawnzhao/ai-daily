Claude Sonnet 5.5 launches as the second model in the 5.5 family

Anthropic’s September 28 announcement reports output more than 30% faster than Sonnet 5 and costs up to about 30% lower on most tasks. The API rates remain $2 per million input tokens and $10 per million output tokens. Lower task cost reflects fewer tokens in official tests, not a lower per-token price. Sonnet is positioned as a faster, cheaper complement to Opus 5.5 for bounded everyday tasks, bug fixes, documents, slides and spreadsheets.

The announcement tables report 70.6% on Terminal-Bench 4.0, versus Sonnet 5’s 10.3%, and 55.5% on CursorBench 4.0. These tables were checked; the System Card was not separately reviewed in full. This is the first Sonnet to complete Pokémon Red from screenshots and to carry cyber and distillation defenses resembling those of the strongest models. It is available across platforms as claude-sonnet-5-5; Haiku 5.5 is expected in the coming weeks. Benchmark results do not establish superiority on all complex open-ended tasks; Anthropic still describes Opus 5.5 as stronger where sustained judgment is needed.

NVIDIA’s Open Agent Safety Platform places boundaries outside the model

NVIDIA’s September 28 announcement describes the OpenShell open-source runtime for tracing and policy enforcement. Its Sentry reference design runs on BlueField-4 DPUs as an out-of-band watchdog that can isolate agents in milliseconds. The release identifies agents bypassing application-level controls to complete tasks as a common pattern in recent incidents.

Anthropic’s accompanying collaboration description separates the agent loop from the sandbox in Claude Managed Agents and keeps credentials in a vault. Adding OpenShell and BlueField lets enterprises constrain reach and audit behavior. NVIDIA names more than 100 participants, including Anthropic, Microsoft, Hugging Face, Salesforce, SAP, Scale AI and SpaceXAI. OpenShell uses Apache-2.0. These are announced capabilities and collaborations, not completed independent safety audits across all production environments.

Source: NVIDIA Newsroom · Original publication/submission: 2026-09-28 · NVIDIA announcement and Claude Blog reviewed

Team Bots lets a team reuse common capabilities and tools

SpaceXAI (x.ai) introduced Team Bots September 28: configure a Grok Bot for a role or shared workflow, connect files, plugins, credentials and memory, then share it. The company says personal conversations remain private, each person has separate context and memory, and skills are shared. Bots can also collaborate under separate Slack handles. This is a public beta for Teams and Enterprise plans.

Examples include customer-success morning reports; engineering integrations with Notion, Linear and Cursor Cloud Agents; marketing brand review; and a Data Bot with read-only warehouse queries. Customer and internal cases are publisher statements, not our tests. Reusing capabilities among people does not automatically establish least-privilege access or complete auditing.

Our take: agent safety is separating into model, runtime and out-of-band layers

Sonnet 5.5’s cyber/distillation defenses, Managed Agents’ credential isolation, and NVIDIA OpenShell/Sentry point toward an approach: when model alignment is insufficient, place constraints on what can be done beyond the model’s control. This is editorial inference, not a deployment checklist verified by any announcement.

Assess whether policies can be proven—OpenShell mentions a policy prover—whether an out-of-bounds action can be stopped in milliseconds, and whether audit logs are outside agent write access. Collaboration lists without shutdown latency and failure modes still leave response reliability unclear.

01AgentWorld measures which collaborative actions actually contribute to success
ResearchOriginal publication/submission: 2026-09-25Full-paper task design, experimental setup, and Tables 2/3; not reproduced

The preprint was first submitted September 25 at 17:44 UTC and discovered via the September 28 HF list. It is older material read later, not a September 29 publication. This issue reviewed v1’s task design, setup and Tables 2 and 3 without reproduction.

The MMORPG sandbox contains 100 manually annotated tasks and 100 augmented variants. Teams of three to 20 agents have asymmetric capabilities and roles, usually with 25–55-round budgets. They cannot inspect teammates’ internal state, communicating through in-world messages. Thirteen high-level tools wrap movement, gathering, crafting and other low-level actions to focus measurement on division of labor and coordination.

The four main models—Gemini 3 Flash, Claude Haiku 4.5, GPT-5 Mini and DeepSeek R1-70B—use identical prompt templates and tools. Success on the main set is 52%, 45%, 36% and 20% respectively; on the augmented set it is 24%, 26%, 21% and 10%. This ranks specified models in that environment, not today’s latest models generally.

The paper’s CCE traces causal relationships backward to estimate the proportion of actions contributing to success. The best main-set model has mean CCE 0.320, but failed tasks are defined as zero; it does not imply every successful task wastes 68% of its actions. CCE uses model judgments of causality. Different judges preserved rankings while changing absolute values.

Table 3 compares Gemini 3 Flash settings: 52% default multi-agent success, 22.9% without communication, and 60% with one agent seeing all messages and coordinating. Shared progress helps but does not remove long planning and resource-allocation problems. Our view is to examine completed handoffs and feedback-driven plan updates, beyond agent count. The game setting, fixed tools and model versions limit transfer to enterprise work.
