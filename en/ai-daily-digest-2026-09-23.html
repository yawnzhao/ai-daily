OpenAI introduces GPT-6 Sol and Luna with lower prices

OpenAI’s announcement has an RSS timestamp of September 22 at 18:00 UTC; the full article was opened. Sol and Luna offer a more affordable route to the Astra generation, using the same approach for professional work, factual tasks, coding, computer use and alignment. Astra remains the strongest overall. The article’s broad headline is about roughly 50% lower prices relative to GPT-5.6 promotional prices; the detailed rates must be read individually.

Per million tokens, Sol input falls from $4 to $2 and output from $20 to $10. Luna input falls from $0.20 to $0.10 and output from $1.20 to $0.50. The last change should not be rounded into an exactly 50% reduction. On AutomationBench 1.0.6, Sol xhigh reports 33.2% at $0.27 per task; Opus 5 max reports 26.9% at 11.1 times the cost. Fable 5.1 with Opus 5 fallback at max reports 31.4%, but the shown cost omits fallback in 40% of tasks and thus understates actual cost.

Other API changes include a 90% discount for cached input reads, cache diagnostics, changing reasoning effort or tool availability without invalidating the cache, and explicit cache breakpoints. Original source: https://openai.com/index/introducing-gpt-6-sol-and-luna/

Bloomberg Law reports a Chinese probe into DeepSeek and Moonshot data practices

The visible Bloomberg Law introduction, timestamped September 22 at 11:27 UTC, reports that the Cyberspace Administration of China was investigating DeepSeek and Moonshot after Anthropic alleged that user data was routed to Claude. It describes visits to offices and interviews with executives and staff, rather than a penalty decision. The accessible introduction and four points were reviewed; the rest was behind a login wall, and The Information’s original report was not opened. Source: https://news.bloomberglaw.com/artificial-intelligence/china-probes-deepseek-moonshot-over-data-breaches-information

Anthropic’s 154-page September 10 threat-intelligence report was read as a source for its allegations, not as proof of regulatory action. It names seven Chinese companies: DeepSeek, Moonshot, Alibaba, Z.ai, SenseTime, MiniMax and Xiaomi. It alleges that DeepSeek, Xiaomi and Moonshot sent their models’ user conversations to Claude and distilled the responses; it reports more than 23 million relevant Moonshot interactions from May through July 2026 and alleges that DeepSeek silently routed some user conversations without their knowledge. These claims remain attributed to Anthropic. Source: https://www.anthropic.com/threat-intelligence-report-september-2026

An official CAC statement and the companies’ responses were not reviewed in this run. Their absence from the review is not a claim that no response exists. Hong Kong stock-price declines are omitted because exchange quotations were not verified.

AI news

Cursor adds rollout monitoring and a security reviewer

Cursor’s September 23 changelog, with an RSS time of 00:00 UTC, was read in full. Rollouts are available to Teams and Enterprise users. Per pull request and environment, the system monitors deployments and labels outcomes healthy, regression or inconclusive. It builds a monitoring plan from the diff, posts it on the PR, and uses deployment triggers plus logs, metrics and traces.

For regressions it points to the relevant change and alerts the team. Configurable follow-up can create a rollback PR or use a cloud agent; this does not mean it automatically merges or deploys the fix. Security Review focuses on exploitable vulnerabilities. Source: https://cursor.com/changelog/rollouts-and-security-reviewer
