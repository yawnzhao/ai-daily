Research Briefs

4 papers
Research overview

Four papers turn expensive processes into decisions about where to spend compute: repository experience becomes reusable skills, models manage long context, evaluation predicts outcomes before trajectories finish, and pruning includes fairness checks. Efficiency and reliability become separate engineering objectives.

Research observation

Declarative Attention, CRISP and EarlyEval use learned control signals to direct resources. Savings can hide rare but consequential errors: missed context, wrong routing or premature termination. Maintain both compute-savings and tail-error records, with a fixed share of full-computation examples as ongoing controls.

Research perspective

When a model decides where to look and whether to keep running, the quality of those decisions starts to determine system efficiency.

01Repo-To-Skill: repository experience becomes reusable skills
ResearchOriginal issue: 2026-09-07Archived edition only; original reading scope is retained in the article. No fresh source verification is claimed.

DisCo distills operational knowledge—configuration, execution order and pitfalls—into reusable, verifiable skills, either across repositories or for a specific task. AREX-Skill Library extracts over 5,000 skills from 1,000 machine-learning repositories, spanning 20 domains and 178 capability types.

With GPT-5.5, the research scaffold and execution budget held fixed, adding skills improves MLE-bench scores by 134.3% and PaperBench by 34.4%, according to the archived account. Reproduction may improve through accumulated skills rather than only larger models or more trials. Evaluate transfer across repositories, frameworks and unfamiliar tasks before adopting the approach.

02Declarative Attention lets models choose what context to inspect
ResearchOriginal issue: 2026-09-07Archived edition only; original reading scope is retained in the article. No fresh source verification is claimed.

Million-token contexts are wasteful when each generated token scans a full KV cache despite needing little history. Declarative Attention lets the model request global browsing, specific regions or recent output. In zero-shot tests of two existing models, total attended tokens fall 52.0% and 31.1%, while accuracy falls 1.27 and 2.75 percentage points; larger models show smaller losses.

This brings retrieval control into the model rather than an external scorer. Quality checks must include region-selection mistakes. Real-traffic latency, throughput and training costs need measurement beyond theoretical savings.

03EarlyEval predicts outcomes before agent trajectories finish
ResearchOriginal issue: 2026-09-07Archived edition only; original reading scope is retained in the article. No fresh source verification is claimed.

Two lightweight LightGBM classifiers predict success and failure with confidence-based early stopping. Across three benchmarks the archived account reports 13%–26% fewer steps, up to 44.1% fewer input tokens and 29.4% fewer output tokens, with 89%–97% prediction accuracy and average solve-rate shifts of one to two percentage points.

Early stopping can combine with smaller evaluation sets, but premature failure labels erase late recoveries and can undervalue long-task correction. Inspect those errors rather than aggregate accuracy alone, retaining complete-trajectory controls to ensure small score shifts do not reorder models.

04Debias-SparseGPT addresses bias introduced by pruning
ResearchOriginal issue: 2026-09-07Archived edition only; original reading scope is retained in the article. No fresh source verification is claimed.

Pruning can amplify persona-dependent answer differences. Debias-SparseGPT uses demographic contrast inputs to constrain representations during pruning. Across generative LLMs at 25%, 50% and structured 2:4 sparsity, it reduces pruning-induced bias relative to SparseGPT while retaining perplexity and zero-shot accuracy.

At the damaging 2:4 setting, longer, richer calibration samples improve both performance and fairness. Compression acceptance should therefore examine bias alongside speed, accuracy and sparsity; calibration content matters.
