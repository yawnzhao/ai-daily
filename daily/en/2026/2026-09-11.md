### Our analysis: platform infrastructure changes the application moat

Cognition’s reported $48 billion valuation emphasized delivered results; OpenAI now exposes agent orchestration, tools, runtimes and memory through infrastructure shared with Codex and ChatGPT for Work. Easier setup can increase platform and token revenue. GPT-Live-1 extends the same infrastructure logic to voice agents.

The issue describes Cognition’s SWE models, including SWE-1.7, and Devin cloud environment as investment in domain data and closed engineering loops. Thin wrappers risk losing differentiation when the platform offers generic infrastructure. Private domain data and workflow integration remain harder to replace, while Financial Services shows a platform also entering vertical applications.

Threat automation, export-control reviews and reported slowdown/antitrust questions make rules part of competition too. The editorial conclusion is that reliable domain workflows, rather than simply building an agent, deserve scrutiny in application valuations. Unconfirmed reports retain their original caveats.

### Research overview

MindTopo probes spatial limits, Mr.LHDR tests long multimodal research, COBRA-Skills reduces optimization costs, and From Parameters to Answers examines internal knowledge retrieval.

<!-- item:papers-context-02 -->
### Research observation

The issue connects measuring capability ceilings, lowering skill-maintenance cost and inspecting model mechanisms. Benchmarks should constrain product claims, while engineering and interpretability clarify what systems can deliver.

<!-- item:papers-context-03 -->
### Research perspective

A best tested deep-research result around forty percent is useful beyond ranking: it sets limits on product promises in that benchmark’s conditions.

<!-- item:papers-01 -->
### MindTopo tests topological reasoning and planning

The archive records 11,030 instances, thirteen task types, five topological properties and fourteen multimodal models. All tested models reason better than they plan, with the strongest still below humans. Supervised and reinforcement learning on Qwen3-VL-2B improve reasoning more than planning.

Connectivity and homeomorphism become structured tests. Separate planning/reasoning measurement exposes different weaknesses; targeted post-training of a small model suggests spatial progress need not come only from scale.

[Reference 1](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.11900)

<!-- item:papers-02 -->
### Mr.LHDR tests multimodal real-world long-horizon research

Eight categories require an average 12.1 intermediate conclusions with dependency depth 10.4. The archived best system reports 43.1% overall accuracy and 34.3% sub-answer accuracy; removing images loses another 12.6 points.

Necessary intermediate conclusions make failures traceable. Visual evidence and long dependency consistency remain bottlenecks in this tested setting, tempering broad Deep Research claims.

[Reference 1](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.11318)

<!-- item:papers-03 -->
### COBRA-Skills makes skill optimization more sample-efficient

Agent-skill evolution is a budget-constrained sequential optimization problem, with contextual bandits guiding evidence-driven changes. Across six heterogeneous agent benchmarks and three target models, it reports the best mean performance, 55%–58% lower optimization cost than SkillOpt and only fifty optimization samples per benchmark.

The issue emphasizes continuing maintenance costs beyond initial skill creation and transfer across tested models, without treating those benchmark results as universal deployment guarantees.

[Reference 1](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.11682)

<!-- item:papers-04 -->
### From Parameters to Answers separates mechanisms of internal knowledge access

Layer interventions across Qwen, Llama and Gemma separate early readability, natural strength, causal steering and late content dependence. Qwen’s pair-conditioned directions retain a late effect, while Llama under the same gating lacks a sustained routing-effect window.

The method distinguishes measurable retrieval mechanisms rather than assuming one pathway across architectures. It provides a framework for knowledge editing and tracing hallucinations, with limited transfer of explanations among model families.

[Reference 1](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.11859)

<!-- item:paper_briefs-05 -->
### Sci-MMR

A scientific multi-hop benchmark with 235 tasks and an average nine charts per task. The archive reports answer accuracy over twenty percent higher than evidence recovery, raising concern about correct answers supported by incorrect evidence.

[Reference 1](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.11243)

<!-- item:paper_briefs-06 -->
### TASCO

Stability-aware test-time adaptation incorporates local stability into confidence estimates to improve reasoning accuracy and token efficiency.

[Reference 1](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.11393)

<!-- item:paper_briefs-07 -->
### SIRF

An industrial content-risk foundation model associated with EMNLP 2026 Industry Track. At 8B it reports 71.3% Black Recall@P95, 15.1 percentage points above baseline, with about seventy million continued-pretraining tokens.

[Reference 1](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.11752)

<!-- item:paper_briefs-08 -->
### When Agents Disagree

Uses Bayesian inverse reasoning to create unlabeled anchors for consistency ranking in multi-agent collective decisions.

[Reference 1](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.11709)
