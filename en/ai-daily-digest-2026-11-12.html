DeepMind's AlphaGenome 2 predicts DNA-variant effects in specific cell types

DeepMind's AlphaGenome 2 predicts DNA-variant effects in specific cell types

Google DeepMind released AlphaGenome 2 on November 11. It predicts how a DNA sequence and its variants affect particular human cell types. Almost every cell carries the same DNA, but heart-muscle and immune cells activate different genes, and a variant can have different consequences in each. AlphaGenome, released last year, predicted many tissue outcomes in parallel, producing tissue-level averages. The new model is conditional, predicting one tissue, cell type or cell state at a time from the same one-million-base DNA input.

DeepMind says training used human and mouse bulk and single-cell data, including more than 107 million single-cell profiles across 68 tissues. On TenK10K, a large atlas of 28 primary immune-cell types, AlphaGenome 2 identified 30% of causal variants, versus 16% for the first-generation model fine-tuned on single-cell data and other specialist tools. For the direction of gene-expression changes, the figures were 43% versus 23%. Without retraining, the model can use single-cell RNA-sequencing snapshots from dozens of cells to predict unseen states. In data from a COVID-19 mRNA-vaccine study, DeepMind says it distinguished activated from resting T cells and showed a variant associated with severe COVID disrupting gene activity more strongly in activated cells. These figures and examples come from DeepMind.

The release also states limits. AlphaGenome 2 is available for noncommercial research through the AlphaGenome API, with commercial research in private preview. Both generations are research-only and lack clinical or diagnostic validation. Genomics lead Žiga Avsec told The Verge that finer predictions cost more compute and would not quickly replace the first model. Applying the new model to September's AlphaGenome Atlas, which covers every possible human single-base change, would be impractical. He said cell-specific resolution matters because drugs target particular proteins and cell types.

Google's Gemini Decisions API returns choices and scores without generating text

Google's Gemini Decisions API returns choices and scores without generating text

Google launched the Gemini Decisions API on November 11, powered by Gemini Decision 1 and available through the Gemini API and Google AI Studio. It evaluates supplied options without text generation. Developers submit text, images, audio, video or PDFs up to 256,000 tokens and as many as 1,000 independent questions. A single forward pass scores candidates and returns probabilities and confidence. Questions can be binary, select one of two to 255 options, or score against an ordered rubric.

Google reports end-to-end latency of 150–250 milliseconds and pricing of $0.10 per million input tokens, with no output-token charges. On the same benchmarks, cost is about one-fifth of using Gemini 3.5 Flash-Lite. The latency figures apply to contexts within 16,000 tokens, remaining broadly flat as questions increase from one to 100. Google claims leading agent-routing, reasoning, ranking and multimodal-understanding results and the lowest calibration error. These are internal tests.

The API targets decisions inside agent systems, such as tool selection, policy compliance, model routing and content scoring, previously handled by specialist classifiers or generative-model calls. Google says Gemini Notebook uses it for real-time routing, reducing everyday-request latency by up to 40%, while Search uses it to add safety checks to MCP tools. Each answer has confidence from zero to one, allowing uncertain cases to go to another model or a person. OpenRouter, LangChain and Vercel integrations are rolling out. Google Cloud support is planned. Today's MMDM paper evaluates this category of model.

United Airlines bans unauthorized third-party AI agents from booking flights

United Airlines bans unauthorized third-party AI agents from booking flights

United Airlines said on November 11 that unauthorized third-party personal AI agents could not book flights for passengers. CBS News quoted its concerns that sharing credentials with assistants could expose sensitive information and lead to unintended purchases, account changes or use of MileagePlus miles. Agent check-in could also violate federal requirements for passengers to personally confirm that baggage contains no prohibited items. United is restricting direct access to its digital platforms and indirect access through agencies and aggregators, and updating booking and ticketing policies.

The Verge says agents may still show United flights but cannot complete transactions. According to The Wall Street Journal, United currently sees little agent booking. CEO Scott Kirby said at a Tuesday event that he did not want another intermediary. United left room for future third-party opportunities once passenger protections are established.

United follows eBay in tightening third-party agent access. Analyst Henry Harteveldt told CBS that United does not want a potentially stronger intermediary on top of Google, comparison sites and online travel agencies, and flight searches themselves cost airlines money. Southwest has taken a different route, letting ChatGPT users view inventory. Harteveldt said United tickets remain bookable through aggregators such as Kayak, Hopper and Google Flights.

CoreWeave funds expansion through prepayments, contract-backed loans and pricier short contracts

CoreWeave funds expansion through prepayments, contract-backed loans and pricier short contracts

Compute-rental provider CoreWeave outlined new financing approaches in third-quarter results released November 10. It is relying more on customer prepayments, borrowing against specific contracts and higher-priced short-term contracts. TechCrunch reports quarterly revenue of $3.66 billion, up 168% year over year, a $709 million net loss and $846 million in quarterly interest expense. Backlog was $132.5 billion. CEO Michael Intrator said the company would not use unsecured debt markets at current yields and did not need to.

CoreWeave expects roughly $9 billion in customer prepayments and deposits over the next three months. More than $3 billion comes from new contracts on already-built capacity, requiring no additional hardware purchases. It raised service prices about 25% in July and another 10% late in the quarter. Intrator put short-term prices above $40 million per megawatt annually, versus $20–25 million for new four-to-five-year contracts. In August, it secured a $2.6 billion delayed-draw loan, DDTL 5.5, designed for short-term customer contracts.

TechCrunch describes the trade-offs. Prepayments ease immediate borrowing pressure but commit supply at fixed prices that may later look low if demand keeps rising. Short contracts offer high prices and faster repricing, while lenders prefer predictable long-term cash flows. Some CoreWeave bond yields exceeded 15% last month. Nebius and IREN also rely on major Microsoft prepayments for construction. Sustainability depends on continued demand and pricing power.

MMDM finds the best exact-match accuracy on multiple-answer questions is only about 37% across 28 decision models2026-11-10 arXiv v1 submission, announced November 11arXiv 2611.14309 v1 HTML abstract, introduction, data construction 3.2, statistics 3.3, experimental setup 4.1, overall results 4.2 and table 2, category results 4.3 and table 3, calibration 4.4, scaling 4.5, cases 4.6, conclusion and limitations reviewed. Appendix not checked section by section. Gemini Decision 1 not among 28 tested models.

Gemini Decision 1 belongs to a model category that returns probabilities over supplied choices in one forward pass without generating text or reasoning. Researchers from the Chinese University of Hong Kong, HKUST and other institutions propose the MMDM benchmark in arXiv 2611.14309 v1. It contains 5,448 questions across 16 task categories, including 4,112 single-choice, 633 multiple-answer and 703 soft-label questions. Option counts range from two to 1,024 and input length reaches 64,817 tokens. Questions rework public datasets and agent trajectories and include new tasks to reduce score inflation from seen questions.

The authors evaluated 28 hosted and locally deployed decision models, excluding Gemini Decision 1 released November 11. Sections 4.2–4.5 and tables 2–3 show WeMM-JEV 27B leading single-choice accuracy at 76.00%, while the best exact-match accuracy across models on multiple-answer questions is 36.81%. Perplexity Decider-V1.2 scores 78.75% on world-knowledge single-choice questions, 53.95% on option logic and 47.95% on constrained planning. Interdependent choices and ordered actions prove harder. Similar accuracy can hide different probability quality. WeMM-JEV and KnowLine differ by less than one percentage point in single-choice accuracy, but KnowLine's soft-label Brier error is almost half. Across five representative models and 3,537 shared questions, OpenJev 4B's highest-confidence bin averages roughly 0.97 confidence and 0.87 accuracy. Rankings also change with input length and option counts.

Limits include text-only inputs and fixed states, questions and option sets, without multimodal input or interactive trajectories. Multiple-answer questions concentrate in option logic, constrained planning, workflows and tool selection. Models may have seen the underlying public source material. Developers using these models for routing, tools or scoring should examine their own task types and confidence reliability alongside overall accuracy.

1
MACaQ quantizes models layer by layer for multiple inference backendsApache-2.0

GitHub organization huawei-csl released MACaQ under Apache 2.0. Quantization reduces weight and activation precision to lower memory and compute requirements. MACaQ independently processes linear layers, requiring only one layer in memory during quantization. It supports llama.cpp with two-, three- and four-bit GGUF, vLLM, Diffusers and PyTorch across text, image, video and speech models.

In arXiv 2611.10649 v1, the authors report quantizing a 122-billion-parameter Qwen3.5 model in 64 minutes on one GPU, using 32.5 GB of GPU memory. On Llama-3-8B, it matches the best DuQuant++ and MR-GPTQ perplexity with eleven times less quantization time. A repository example runs Qwen3.8-27B in vLLM with four-bit weights and activations, increasing perplexity from 2.8463 to 2.8994 while multiplying prefill and decode speed by 2.33 and 2.58. These are author tests. Limits include inability to compensate errors across layers and roughly 8% inference overhead on the smallest 0.6B model.

The repository was created November 10 and the paper submitted November 7. Teams running models locally or on their own clusters can compare these claims on their own models.
