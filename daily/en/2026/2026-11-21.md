### Anthropic closes its IPO. Who gets the $93.5 billion?

Anthropic began trading on Nasdaq on November 19. Its announcement on November 20 concerned the closing of the offering. The underwriters exercised their option in full, buying another 147 million shares at $83 each. That brought the public offering to 1.127 billion shares of Class A common stock and approximately $93.5 billion in gross proceeds, before underwriting discounts, commissions and offering expenses.

About $91.6 billion in gross proceeds came from shares sold by Anthropic. The rest belongs to stockholders who sold existing shares. At the same time, seven companies bought Class N common stock at the same price per share in a separate private placement worth approximately $26 billion. Combined gross proceeds of roughly $119.5 billion include the selling stockholders’ portion. The first-day share price, the company’s valuation, the gross proceeds and the cash the company takes home after expenses are four different measures.

### Two proposed proofs of the Riemann hypothesis await scrutiny

In a personal statement, mathematician Levent Alpöge says he and Claude found a proof of the Riemann hypothesis and formalized the argument. He describes the number-theory tools he had accumulated over many years, the simpler problems he explored for ideas and the model’s role in connecting parts of the argument. The hypothesis bears on how precisely mathematicians can describe the distribution of prime numbers.

TechCrunch reports that another mathematician, Alessandro Masullo, has circulated an independent draft. According to Ravi Vakil, that work did not use AI. Each claim needs its own examination. Do the formal files pass verification? Does the proposition they encode match the original problem? Can other mathematicians follow and check the argument? The announcement does not establish that the mathematical community has accepted either proof. The order in which the drafts appeared tells us little about the relative merits of the methods.

### Signal restricts mobile assistants’ access to chat screens

Signal’s Meredith Whittaker explains what changes when an encrypted message reaches its destination. End-to-end encryption protects a message in transit. Once the recipient reads it, the text is on the screen. If an operating-system assistant can read that screen, it could process the contents of the conversation.

Signal says it restricts screenshots in the app on iOS 27. On Android it takes action where it can detect an assistant trying to read the screen. Users can turn off the protection in settings to restore screenshots. The measure addresses screen capture. Other forms of on-device access remain possible, and Signal says it cannot provide the same protection on macOS. It is asking platform operators to document access mechanisms and give apps an explicit way to opt out.

[Signal](https://signal.org/blog/protecting-your-privacy/)

### Amazon Textract Insights points reviewers to the page behind an extracted value

AWS announced general availability of Amazon Textract Insights. Users describe the fields they need in natural language and submit a document or a packet containing several kinds of documents. An asynchronous request returns structured results with a confidence score and a source page for each value. Users do not have to train a separate model for each layout.

A loan application might combine pay slips, bank statements and a handwritten explanation in one scan. A reviewer who finds an income figure still needs to know which pay slip it came from and what period it covers. The page reference shortens the search, and the score can help prioritize review. Neither alone guarantees the value is correct. At launch the service accepts PDF, PNG, JPEG and TIFF files and charges per page. Chinese is absent from the six listed document languages.

[AWS](https://aws.amazon.com/blogs/machine-learning/announcing-amazon-textract-insights-verifiable-generative-ai-for-documents/)

### Hebbia tests citations and slides on investment-banking research tasks

In a customer case study published by OpenAI, Hebbia tested GPT-6 Astra on its own financial research tasks. ### OpenAI describes safeguards against unauthorized model distillation

OpenAI describes how it protects internal reasoning information in individual requests and looks across the service for signs of coordinated activity. Its concern is that a party could use model outputs to train another system that reproduces some capabilities without retaining the original safeguards. The company describes model training, service-level protections and investigations as parts of its response. Its account does not establish that the risk has been eliminated, and knowledge distillation has legitimate uses.

### How often does repeated reliance on an AI companion coincide with observable harm?

Viet Cuong Nguyen and colleagues analyzed Character.AI and Replika conversations donated by 110 English-speaking adults. They coded behavioral signs of emotional overreliance separately from observable relational harm. Among non-roleplay conversations, 63.2% showed signs of the former and 10.5% showed the latter. Both percentages count conversations. Neither tells us what proportion of participants experienced harm.

Behaviors such as repeated requests for support sometimes coincided with harm. Changes in people’s lives outside the chats, unspoken distress and patterns spanning longer periods may be missing from the records. Participants volunteered, the time spans of their records varied, and this observational design cannot establish causality. The authors suggest looking for recurring or escalating patterns. The preprint was submitted on November 19 at 16:46 Beijing time and is included under the 48-hour research window.

### Inkling 2 Mini takes repeated passes through the same middle layers

Thinking Machines Lab’s Inkling 2 Mini accepts images and text. According to the Hugging Face technical post, the mixture-of-experts model has roughly 26 billion physical parameters and activates about 3 billion per token. The middle 12 layers run three times with the same weights. Repeated passes add computation while reusing the stored weights. Counting each visit as a new set of parameters would overstate the model’s size.

The model card lists a context window of up to one million tokens and the Apache-2.0 license. Suitability for a particular computer depends on precision, memory and context length. The post lists supported frameworks and demonstrates ways to run the model. Accuracy, latency and cost still need to be measured on the intended task.

[Hugging Face](https://huggingface.co/blog/inkling2mini)
