### Ten thousand agents and a longstanding mathematics problem: credit needs rules too

The claimed proof is a large-scale research demonstration, with task decomposition, 2.7 million messages and 130 billion tokens, rather than one model answering one question. The priority dispute concerns timing: Buckmaster and Alpöge say they had substantial progress in normal academic exchange; the allegation is that OpenAI redirected agents after hearing it.

The analysis asks how academic priority rules cope with machine research completed in days. Attribution must address human ideas entering AI-generated work; declining the prize cannot replace rules. Peer review takes time that compressed research cycles do not automatically provide. Agent messages become experimental records, whose completeness and independent review matter.

The issue connects this to slowdown calls, distillation allegations and consumer-agent privacy: capability gains expose new governance gaps, now including mathematics’ longstanding priority conventions. These are editorial questions, not verified findings about misconduct.

### Research perspective

<!-- item:papers-01 -->
### Procedural Graphs make execution knowledge editable

The method represents how to perform tasks through procedure–relation–procedure triples, analogous to factual knowledge graphs. Each decision locates active nodes; a guiding model turns the subgraph into contextual instructions. An LLM refiner compares successful and failed trajectories and edits topology and properties, including flawed expert priors.

This turns experience into reusable structure for failures such as getting lost, repeating actions or confusing order, beyond simply remembering facts.

[Reference 1](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.09153)

<!-- item:papers-02 -->
### ExecCritic separates testing from repair

A Test Agent independently creates repository-native tests, filtered fail-closed and then frozen. A Repair Agent changes code from execution feedback without editing the tests. On SWE-bench Verified, high-quality tests alone raise repair from 61.2% to 65.3%; fine-tuning two Qwen-3.5-35B roles reaches 72.6%, 11.4 percentage points above the no-test baseline.

The archived lesson is to prevent self-written tests from creating false confidence. Frozen tests keep repair from quietly changing the acceptance criteria, with medium open models delivering much of the reported benefit.

[Reference 1](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.09133)

<!-- item:papers-03 -->
### MeClear identifies harmful memories through cooperative attribution

Long-task memory can accumulate outdated, conflicting and misleading information. Sampled cooperative Shapley attribution identifies negative-utility memories for selective suppression. Across ten long-conversation memory pools, the archive reports 85.9% target recall and 82.3% task recovery, 25.5 percentage points above a leave-one-out baseline.

More memory is not always better. Task-conditioned removal avoids indiscriminate forgetting and complements PlanFence: one manages stale plans, the other stale memories.

[Reference 1](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.09115)

<!-- item:papers-04 -->
### Contrastive Evidence Exploration goes beyond one-shot multi-hop retrieval

As intermediate entities expose new evidence, this training-free framework creates contrastive facets for passages offline, then iteratively retrieves evidence, generates probing queries and refines relevance before coverage-aware ranking. The archived account reports evaluation on MuSiQue, HotpotQA and 2WikiMultihopQA and acceptance at CIKM 2026.

It can attach to existing RAG pipelines; facets help judge relevance, while coverage-aware ranking reduces clusters of redundant evidence.

[Reference 1](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.07050)
