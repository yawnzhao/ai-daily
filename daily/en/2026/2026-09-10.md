### China’s commerce ministry rejects the US distillation allegations

In its response to joint notice AA26-251A, the ministry characterizes the allegations as unsupported in fact and law. It describes distillation as a neutral, widely used technique, including by US firms whose research reports describe distilling Chinese models. It objects to conflating company and investor interests with national security and endorsing unfair user-agreement terms.

The ministry offers constructive discussion under the intergovernmental AI dialogue agreed by the two countries’ leaders while promising countermeasures against suppression framed as anti-distillation action. This is a direct follow-up to the previous issue’s notice; the positions are paraphrased, not fabricated direct quotations.

### Our analysis: application results and general models face different pricing pressures

The issue contrasts Cognition’s reported $26 billion to $48 billion valuation and $492 million to nearly $900 million run-rate revenue with reported Z.ai and MiniMax declines. Coding agents sell delivered software work—planning, writing, testing and deployment—rather than raw tokens; reliability can matter to result-based revenue. Anthropic’s reported heavy-agent savings also support application economics.

The analysis sees DeepSeek’s claimed cheaper, stronger Flash version encouraging price competition, concerns about model margins and capital concentrating at leading firms. The proposed IPO remains a media claim. It links the CISA notice and commerce-ministry response to competition over rules as well as markets.

The possibility that model access becomes a utility while applications capture value is editorial interpretation. Unverified share prices and financing figures do not independently establish that value-chain outcome.

The archived financial comparison reports Z.ai down 7.88% and MiniMax down 6.73% that day, with month-to-date retreats of 29.58% and 14.27% respectively. These remain reported market figures. The named price-pressure example is DeepSeek V4.1 Flash; Anthropic’s cited heavy-agent saving is up to 45%.

### Research overview

Φ-Bench evaluates infrastructure engineering, Strangers to Themselves questions self-reports, VLX-VR adds memory-based video observation, and Feyospace describes specialized cyber models from a seven-person team.

<!-- item:papers-context-02 -->
### Research observation

The issue connects open weights and domain data, automated infrastructure work, behavioral evaluation rather than self-description, and video-agent memory loops. Capability diffusion requires engineering discipline as responsibilities become distributed.

<!-- item:papers-context-03 -->
### Research perspective

The archive interprets small-team training and LLM infrastructure work as signs that frontier systems increasingly involve practical engineering beyond large laboratories.

<!-- item:papers-01 -->
### Φ-Bench evaluates engineering the infrastructure that powers LLMs

Tasks span kernel-level function completion to end-to-end system optimization, measuring capability boundaries across the stack. A layered benchmark enables comparison and can reveal differences between local fixes and global optimization. It supplies a baseline for AI-assisted improvement of AI infrastructure, rather than proof of reliable autonomous operation.

[Reference 1](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.10226)

<!-- item:papers-02 -->
### Strangers to Themselves finds weak correspondence between self-reports and behavior

Across nine behavioral evaluations, the archive reports correlation r=+0.04 between model self-report and actual behavior. Giving full question items raises it to +0.24, while asking about a generic AI agent yields +0.28. First-person framing introduces a favorable self-description bias.

Model self-reports should not replace behavioral tests, especially in personalization, safety review, recommendations, risk management or psychological applications.

[Reference 1](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.09899)

<!-- item:papers-03 -->
### VLX-VR adds a Think–Memory–Observation loop to video reasoning

The model writes key observations to external memory while viewing and revisits evidence as needed. The archive reports 78.79% on MINERVA, then described as state of the art, cross-duration accuracy variance of 2.97 percentage-points-squared, and 96.20% alignment of reasoning trajectories with references.

External memory and active revisiting address long-video context limits. Stability across duration and inspectable trajectories are relevant to monitoring and embodied applications; these remain historical reported metrics.

[Reference 1](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.09985)

<!-- item:papers-04 -->
### Feyospace-v1 describes cyber models trained by seven independent researchers

The team builds a 164,269-trajectory dataset and open-weight security agents, reporting a 23.76% CyberGym improvement. feyospace-s1 reaches 63.24% verified success and tenth place on the archived ranking.

The issue views domain trajectories and open weights as a route for small teams toward specialized capability. The dataset itself is a research contribution; the results are not a current ranking or our reproduction.

[Reference 1](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.08418)

<!-- item:paper_briefs-05 -->
### FOM-UL

Uses forget-to-retain salience for layer-selective unlearning, reporting improvements over GA/NPO on TOFU, KnowUnDo and MUSE, with retained unlearning effects under 8-bit and 4-bit quantization.

[Reference 1](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.10439)

<!-- item:paper_briefs-06 -->
### SocialRL

Uses multi-turn reinforcement learning and six-dimensional process rewards for social intelligence, reporting a mean 9.2-percentage-point goal-achievement gain across social-dialogue benchmarks.

[Reference 1](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.09764)

### WRP

Estimates layer redundancy from checkpoint weights for depth pruning without forward passes or calibration data, approaching activation-based methods in the archived account.

[Reference 1](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.09883)

Direct diversity optimization in preference post-training reports the highest task success and successful-strategy coverage on BabyAI, BabaIsAI and WebShop in its tested comparison.

[Reference 1](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.10052)
