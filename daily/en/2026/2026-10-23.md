### Agent Skills moves to a neutral foundation, and the skill standard enters shared governance

On October 22, Anthropic announced that it is donating the Agent Skills standard to the Agentic AI Foundation, part of the Linux Foundation. The transfer includes the name and logo, the GitHub organization, and the agentskills.io domain. According to the announcement, engineers from several companies will maintain the standard as individuals. Using or contributing to the standard does not require joining the foundation first.

A skill here is a folder that can hold a SKILL.md file, scripts, or reference material, so an agent gets the steps and domain knowledge for a kind of task. It is not a new capability of one model, and it does not certify every third-party skill as safe. The company's later roadmap includes versioned releases, a formal proposal process, and security guidance for writing and installing skills.

The news is that governance of a portable way of working is starting to move from a single vendor to a neutral organization. Teams will need to watch the specification version, the maintainers, and execution permissions, not only collect prompts. An open standard and whether a particular skill is safe to run are two different judgments.

[Anthropic News | October 22, 2026](https://www.anthropic.com/news/donating-agent-skills-to-the-agentic-ai-foundation)

### Anthropic discusses folding subagents back so shared context stays in the main loop

In an October 22 engineering article, Anthropic argues that some subagents split by business function can gradually be folded into skills and tools called by the main agent. With progressive loading, the system first offers a short description and loads the concrete procedure and tools only when they are needed, instead of stuffing a large context in at the start. The article is about agents built with Claude and Claude Code.

The authors say that once knowledge for a continuous task is split across several contexts, a summary handoff can drop detail, and a local evaluation may not represent completion of the final task. They suggest building an end-to-end evaluation first, then merging one boundary at a time, and comparing quality, latency, and specific failures. Tasks with a lot of shared context are the first candidates for merging. Independent research, components with a clear interface, and black-box checks may still be better kept apart.

This is architecture advice, not an experimental verdict that multi-agent systems have failed. The article also reports that a merge once broke a flow that had been working, so an average score is not enough, and a check should not simply be deleted. Permissions, budgets, and approval thresholds still have to be enforced by the tools and the runtime.

[Claude Blog | October 22, 2026](https://claude.com/resources/articles/building-agents-when-to-fold-subagents-into-your-main-agent-loop)

### Google expands a virtual-power-plant partnership to ease grid pressure through demand response

On October 22, Google and Voltus announced an expanded partnership to build a 1-gigawatt virtual power plant in the PJM grid region of the United States, ten times the earlier 100-megawatt agreement. This is aggregated adjustable capacity, not a 1-gigawatt generator already in operation.

Voltus aggregates flexible resources such as smart thermostats, batteries, and industrial equipment at businesses and homes, temporarily reducing demand at peaks. Participants receive bill savings or cash. Google is providing funding for the project. The text announces the expanded agreement. It does not give a timetable for when the full capacity will actually be in place.

This is another way to look at the expansion of AI compute: besides adding supply, shifting when electricity is used can ease pressure. Whether the callable load is really available, how long it lasts, and whether participants can respond still decide the practical value of this kind of resource. Agreement capacity should not be written as a solved power gap for data centers.

[Google AI Blog | October 22, 2026](https://blog.google/innovation-and-ai/infrastructure-and-cloud/global-network/virtual-power-plant-1-gigawatt/)

### NVIDIA introduces NVIDIA Minerva, putting the machine room and the jobs on one view

On October 22, NVIDIA introduced NVIDIA Minerva, combining telemetry from GPUs, networks, storage, schedulers, and AI jobs so operations teams can locate anomalies and possible faults. It uses a dynamic knowledge graph to relate infrastructure and the jobs that are running, and it groups many related alerts into a more concentrated incident.

The publisher describes asking about cluster state in natural language and receiving repair suggestions. A team can keep a person in the loop, or connect automation through an infrastructure interface. IREN is listed as a user. Some other partners are planned deployments, so the whole partner list should not be counted as already running.

The focus is the useful work a machine room actually produces, not only how many GPUs were purchased. A training job that slows down may come from the network, storage, or a local hardware state. A cross-layer link explains more than an isolated count. The announcement does not prove the same fault prediction and repair benefit in every environment.

### A global forest-type map at 10 meters separates natural forest from planted uses

On October 22, Google DeepMind, Google Research, and partner research groups released the Forest Typology 2020 global map. The Chinese edition also uses the short form ForTy. At a 10-meter scale it separates primary forest, naturally regenerating forest, planted forest, intensive plantation, tree crops and agroforestry, and other land. The team also shared an early year-by-year change sequence from 2017 through 2025.

The question is not simply whether trees are present. It is how the land is used, and what replaced a forest afterward. The model combines satellite time series with AlphaEarth Foundations representations, and uses planting patterns, growth cycles, and harvest changes to separate the classes.

The team reports that, in expert labels at more than 9,000 random sites, forest-type classification was about 90 percent correct and the forest versus non-forest distinction was about 95 percent correct. Those are overall results for that validation design, not a claim that every parcel has the same accuracy. The map also provides a probability for each class. A specific use still needs a chosen threshold and local evidence. This issue read the research groups' introduction. It did not review a technical article as the full paper or treat the map as an automatic compliance decision.

[Google DeepMind | October 22, 2026](https://deepmind.google/blog/beyond-tree-cover-mapping-the-worlds-forest-types/)

OlmoEarth v1.3, released on October 22, provides precomputed global annual features and a browser explorer for those embeddings. ### Granite Timeseries Ensemble uses several models to give a prediction range

On October 22, an IBM research team introduced Granite Timeseries Ensemble. It combines four complementary architectures for forecasting a series over time. The members are Granite forecasting models. The recommended default gives the members equal influence, merges the forecasts in probability space, and then produces a median and quantiles. Another option weights members adaptively by the width of the prediction interval.

The recipe stores the ensemble configuration. It does not fuse the four model weights into one model. The first run still downloads the member checkpoints. The text warns that a narrower prediction interval is not necessarily more accurate. It can also be overconfidence. Users should compare the options on representative data, rather than treat a nominal coverage rate as reliability already achieved in every setting.

This issue's scope is the official technical article and its code examples. The code was not installed, and the result is not claimed as reproduced. The license and use conditions of each member model still have to be checked for the models actually used.

[IBM Research | October 22, 2026](https://huggingface.co/blog/ibm-research/granite-timeseries-ensemble-r1)
