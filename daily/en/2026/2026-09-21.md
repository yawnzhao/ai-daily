### Research observation: evaluation methods are themselves under review

CogGym uses human behavioral consistency, SWE-Proof formal verification, PIR internal states and RecreationWorld mixed GUI/code environments. Translation-reasoning and SFT studies show protocol choices affecting conclusions. These methods address different aspects of evidence rather than offering one accepted external-certification standard.

<!-- item:papers-01 -->
### CogGym compares fifty models with human behavior

A human-in-the-loop pipeline turns experiments from one hundred papers into a task-independent Experimental Markup Language, standardizing 258 cognitive-science experiments. Larger/newer models fit humans better, but commonsense progress lags formal mathematics/code benchmarks.

Human split-half reliability is 0.93/0.95/0.92 for text/image/video; best-model behavioral fits are 0.59/0.58/0.43. These quantities have distinct meanings; the comparison highlights gaps without treating fit as identical to reliability or universal intelligence.

[Reference 1](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.21259)

<!-- item:papers-02 -->
### SWE-Proof uses machine-checkable patch proofs

Benchproofer converts SWE-bench Verified tasks with known-correct patches into five hundred formal-verification tasks: specifications for new code, axiomatized summaries for called functions and mechanical/adversarial acceptance gates. It addresses incomplete tests and memorization concerns; proof validity remains tied to the constructed specifications and assumptions.

[Reference 1](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.21190)

<!-- item:papers-03 -->
### PIR reads recognition of withheld answers from internal states

Adapts concealed-information testing by presenting questions and candidate answers and identifying internal recognition, without an honest reference model or labeled corpus. Across eight Gemma/Qwen/Llama/Mistral/Phi models, withheld-answer recovery exceeds 0.70 in reported settings. This is an experimental technique, not a universal model lie detector.

[Reference 1](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.21996)

<!-- item:papers-04 -->
### RecreationWorld tests mixed computer-use agents

Agents discover a running reference implementation’s behavior and reproduce it through GUI actions plus coding tools across Ubuntu, macOS, Windows, Android and web. The reference acts as an oracle for hidden behavioral tests and execution-grounded rewards, giving a controlled environment for mixed workflows rather than authorization to act in external systems.

[Reference 1](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.22000)

<!-- item:papers-05 -->
### Apple researchers analyze when reasoning helps machine translation

Across models, languages, domains and datasets, preferred reasoning language varies and length/quality are nonmonotonic. Hierarchical Meta-Summarization derives understanding/planning, translation/drafting and revision/checking phases without predefined categories. Reasoning should be deliberately adapted to model, length and mode rather than assumed beneficial everywhere.

[Reference 1](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.21247)

<!-- item:papers-06 -->
### SFT next-turn gains do not transfer to autonomous workflows in tested tasks

Qwen3 4B/14B and Gemma3 4B/12B improve under gold-history next-turn prediction for multi-turn customer-service workflows, yet none improves autonomous execution. Strict full-trajectory success peaks at only 10.4%. Protocol mismatch explains the reported evaluation gap; the result does not establish that all SFT is ineffective.

[Reference 1](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.21187)
