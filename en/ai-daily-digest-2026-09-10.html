China’s commerce ministry rejects the US distillation allegations

In its response to joint notice AA26-251A, the ministry characterizes the allegations as unsupported in fact and law. It describes distillation as a neutral, widely used technique, including by US firms whose research reports describe distilling Chinese models. It objects to conflating company and investor interests with national security and endorsing unfair user-agreement terms.

The ministry offers constructive discussion under the intergovernmental AI dialogue agreed by the two countries’ leaders while promising countermeasures against suppression framed as anti-distillation action. This is a direct follow-up to the previous issue’s notice; the positions are paraphrased, not fabricated direct quotations.

Our analysis: application results and general models face different pricing pressures

The issue contrasts Cognition’s reported $26 billion to $48 billion valuation and $492 million to nearly $900 million run-rate revenue with reported Z.ai and MiniMax declines. Coding agents sell delivered software work—planning, writing, testing and deployment—rather than raw tokens; reliability can matter to result-based revenue. Anthropic’s reported heavy-agent savings also support application economics.

The analysis sees DeepSeek’s claimed cheaper, stronger Flash version encouraging price competition, concerns about model margins and capital concentrating at leading firms. The proposed IPO remains a media claim. It links the CISA notice and commerce-ministry response to competition over rules as well as markets.

The possibility that model access becomes a utility while applications capture value is editorial interpretation. Unverified share prices and financing figures do not independently establish that value-chain outcome.

The archived financial comparison reports Z.ai down 7.88% and MiniMax down 6.73% that day, with month-to-date retreats of 29.58% and 14.27% respectively. These remain reported market figures. The named price-pressure example is DeepSeek V4.1 Flash; Anthropic’s cited heavy-agent saving is up to 45%.

02Strangers to Themselves finds weak correspondence between self-reports and behavior
ResearchOriginal issue: 2026-09-10Archived edition only; original reading scope is retained in the article. No fresh source verification is claimed.

Across nine behavioral evaluations, the archive reports correlation r=+0.04 between model self-report and actual behavior. Giving full question items raises it to +0.24, while asking about a generic AI agent yields +0.28. First-person framing introduces a favorable self-description bias.

Model self-reports should not replace behavioral tests, especially in personalization, safety review, recommendations, risk management or psychological applications.

03VLX-VR adds a Think–Memory–Observation loop to video reasoning
ResearchOriginal issue: 2026-09-10Archived edition only; original reading scope is retained in the article. No fresh source verification is claimed.
