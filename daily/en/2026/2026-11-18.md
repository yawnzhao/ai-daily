# AI Daily, Issue 73 — When AI starts pressing buttons for you

Wednesday, November 18, 2026, Beijing time.

GPT-6.2 targets professional work, Gemini takes on everyday tasks, and Microsoft and Salesforce connect customer systems. This issue also looks at data preparation, desktop permissions and why fairness audits need to be revisited as models change.

### GPT-6.2 puts the quality of professional deliverables first

OpenAI has released GPT-6.2 Astra and GPT-6.2 Sol for complex professional projects and everyday work, respectively. The announcement emphasizes the quality of documents, presentations, code and multistep task outputs, along with the resources needed to produce them. OpenAI says the models are available from launch to eligible paid users of ChatGPT Work and Codex, and to API developers.

For users, the useful question is whether the output is closer to something they can deliver: can figures in a document be traced to their sources, does a presentation follow the supplied template, and do code changes meet the actual requirements? The value of an upgrade should be assessed through specific tasks and the amount of revision they require. A leaderboard alone cannot settle that question for every kind of work.

[OpenAI](https://openai.com/index/introducing-gpt-6-2-astra-sol)

### Gemini takes on appointments and shopping, with explicit authorization for critical steps

Google is expanding Gemini's task features free of charge to the United States, Japan, South Korea, Canada, Australia and India, using the Gemini 4 Hydrogen model. Some advanced features are launching first in the U.S. Users can switch to task mode in the app and use email, calendars and other connected services to complete multistep tasks.

The announcement gives examples including booking car maintenance, analyzing electricity bills and shopping. The calling assistant identifies itself to businesses as AI, and businesses can opt out. Shopping supports preset spending limits and requires confirmation or prior authorization before a purchase. Actions such as adding everyday items to a shopping cart can separately be set to always allow. Adding an item to a list and paying for it remain separate permissions.

Google says each user's tasks run in a dedicated, hardware-isolated virtual machine, with prompt-injection filtering and web safety checks. The company also acknowledges that some websites still require the assistant to hand the screen back to the user for the final step. The practical test is how well workflows connect across apps and how clearly progress is preserved when a task stops, beyond whether a demonstration succeeds in one attempt.

[Google](https://blog.google/innovation-and-ai/products/gemini-app/tasks-in-gemini-free-new-features/)

<!-- item:copilotforce-customer-actions -->
### Microsoft and Salesforce connect customer context, while writebacks require confirmation

Microsoft and Salesforce have announced Copilotforce, a partnership that brings Salesforce customer data, sales workflows and business actions into Copilot, Teams and Microsoft Fabric. The Salesforce in Copilot plugin is in public preview with more than 38 prebuilt sales skills. It combines customer records with meetings, email and documents to support opportunity analysis, follow-up drafts and assessments of renewal risk.

Permissions remain under the existing systems' control. Salesforce governs customer data access and supported business actions, while Microsoft continues to apply enterprise controls to communications and documents. Writing changes back to the CRM requires explicit user confirmation and must respect existing validation rules. A possible development mentioned in a meeting and a formal change to a customer record remain distinct steps.

Availability varies across the integrations. Data federation from Microsoft OneLake into Salesforce Data 360 is generally available, while sharing in the other direction remains in public preview. Sales Cloud in Teams is currently an enterprise pilot. The data connections are intended to reduce the need for duplicate datasets; the partnership does not mean every capability is already broadly available.

[Microsoft](https://news.microsoft.com/source/2026/11/17/microsoft-and-salesforce-announce-copilotforce-bringing-trusted-customer-context-and-action-into-microsoft-ai-experiences/)

### Lumo arrives on the desktop with separate controls for automatic, approved and blocked actions

Proton has launched Lumo Desktop for Windows, macOS and Linux, according to TechCrunch. It can connect to file systems, apps, and local or remote tools. Users can decide, for each connector, which actions run automatically, which require approval and which are blocked. Individual tools can also be disabled, and scheduled tasks can have their own permissions.

Proton says conversations and connected content are not used for personalized advertising or model training. Connecting a desktop assistant to an app does not authorize every operation within it. Permissions to read files, modify files and execute commands need to be understood separately.

[TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2026/11/17/protons-lumo-a-privacy-focused-alternative-to-chatgpt-and-claude-arrives-on-desktop/)

### Gemini separates history and training settings as temporary-chat retention increases

Google will gradually extend the retention of temporary chats and conversations made with chat history turned off from 72 hours to about two months over the coming weeks, TechCrunch reports. Citing updated documentation, the report says these temporary conversations are not used for model training; retention serves purposes including service delivery, feedback and security.

Chat history and AI training will also have separate settings. Both new settings will default to off for users who previously disabled Keep Activity. Users who had it enabled will retain chat history and can decide separately whether to allow training. Whether a conversation can be retrieved in the interface, whether it is used for training and how long it remains on the server are different questions.

[TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2026/11/17/google-to-keep-gemini-chats-for-two-months-up-from-72-hours-even-with-history-off/)

### SageMaker connects data generation, quality checks and human review

AWS has added data preparation capabilities to Amazon SageMaker AI, including automatic labeling, synthetic data generation, quality assessment, transformation and human review. For tasks with fixed answer sets, such as classification, agreement among several models can provide a confidence score. Records below a threshold enter a human review queue. Agreement does not replace domain judgment.

The announcement uses a customer-support evaluation as an example. If a product guide states that a factory reset erases saved settings, generated reference answers must retain that warning. Data checks need to identify duplicates, noise and answer leakage, as well as conditions that could change a user's decision.

The capabilities are generally available in the US East (N. Virginia) and US West (Oregon) regions. Source data, generated datasets and labels remain in the user's own storage buckets, with execution permissions under the user's control. Generated specifications and code can be inspected and edited.

[AWS](https://aws.amazon.com/blogs/machine-learning/announcing-ai-data-preparation-capabilities-in-amazon-sagemaker-build-high-quality-datasets-for-model-development-workflows/)

### Spotify opens profile protection to all artists, with an option to approve releases first

Spotify is rolling out Artist Profile Protection to all artists, TechCrunch reports. Artists can receive alerts when music is submitted under their names and remove the association with their profiles if the work does not belong to them.

Releases go live as scheduled by default. An advanced setting lets artists require manual approval before a release appears on their profiles. The tool addresses misattribution caused by impersonation, shared names and metadata errors. It is not equivalent to a system that detects all AI-generated music.

[TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2026/11/17/spotifys-new-tool-aims-to-stop-deepfakes-from-being-attributed-to-real-artists/)

### A fairness audit can age even when the model name stays the same

Why read this now? As model updates and enterprise integrations accelerate, deployments must address whether an earlier evaluation still describes today's service. In the preprint Auditing a Moving Target, submitted on November 16, Mika Facchini studies simulated venture screening. The study compares how provider-served models handle synthetic founder profiles with different names, origins and university backgrounds over time.

The research spans 13 months, three sampling occasions and five model providers, with 248,400 API calls. In the first study, the same prompts were sent to the same dated model identifier a year later, and the overall pattern of bias changed. Section 4.2 and Table 4 support a difference in the overall pattern under inference clustered by name. A significant change in an individual effect at the call level does not establish that every bias changed significantly.

Sections 3.4–3.5 and 8.4 also limit the interpretation. Early calls did not record the actual served-model string; some comparisons between model generations are confounded with a year of elapsed time; and human raters did not validate the names' perceived origins. Differences in output cannot establish whether the serving configuration changed or the underlying model was replaced. The experiment concerns simulated investment decisions, not actual lending or hiring outcomes.

The author recommends recording requested identifiers, response identifiers, prompts and evaluation settings, and repeating audits when models change. The paper was submitted at 11:10 UTC on November 16, within this issue's 36-hour source-catalog window. It is included in the body under the 48-hour supplementary window, with its original submission date retained.

### Concourse: why can individually sharp video frames flicker when played together?

The November 16 episode of Latent Space features Black Forest Labs researchers Andreas Blattmann and Dustin Podell discussing how images, video and sound jointly train visual intelligence. The episode is listed in Concourse. This account draws on the publisher's written introduction about moving cameras and video upscaling, rather than a complete transcript.

The introduction describes two examples. As a camera moves around a person, the model must produce angles that were previously unseen. When frames are sharpened individually, each may look good, yet different decisions about details can cause flicker during playback. Temporal consistency is therefore a requirement for video upscaling; the team's upscaler uses the FLUX 3 backbone.

Read alongside today's professional models and cross-app assistants, the discussion shifts attention from a strong individual output to consistency across steps. Keeping names and figures consistent in documents, records aligned across enterprise systems and objects continuous in video are different technical problems, but each affects whether the finished result is ready to use. This is the connection drawn in this issue; the episode itself discusses visual models.

### vLLM 0.34.0 changes cache defaults: check compatibility before upgrading

vLLM has released v0.34.0, removing the old GPU model runner in favor of the new runner. The default cache block size changes from 16 to 64, and extensible key-value caching is enabled by default. The new approach allocates the cache based on memory actually available after graph capture and warmup. Maintainers say most models can obtain larger caches and higher throughput.

Benefits and costs depend on deployment conditions. The release notes also report increased per-token decoding latency for some models on H200 hardware under particular high-concurrency conditions. Handling more requests per unit of time does not mean every step of every request is faster.

The upgrade notes list dependency and compatibility changes, including the removal of ROCm 7.2 build artifacts and higher requirements for dependencies such as huggingface_hub. The project uses the Apache-2.0 license. This issue checked the release notes and license; it did not deploy or run the software locally.
